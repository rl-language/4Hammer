## Modifiers — 02.02.01

Modifiers are cumulative. Apply replacements first, then multiplication, addition, division, subtraction, and round fractions up once after all operations. A replacement characteristic of `0`, `-` or `*` cannot be modified further. Characteristics of `-`, `*` or `N/A` cannot be modified.

Improving WS/BS/Sv/Ld subtracts from the threshold; worsening adds. Improving AP subtracts from its numeric value; worsening adds, to a maximum of zero. Improving/worsening ordinary unsigned characteristics adds/subtracts. A model's WS/BS modifier applies to every equipped weapon.

After all characteristic modifiers: M/Range at least 1 inch; T/A/S/D at least 1; Sv/InSv at least 2+; Ld 5+ through 8+; OC at least zero and never changed to `-`; WS/BS 2+ through 6+; AP at most zero. Special/unmodifiable values are preserved rather than passed through those numeric bounds.

Dice modifiers apply after rerolls. “Unmodified” means the result after rerolls but before modifiers. A modified result may exceed the die's maximum; results below one become one. Sum hit/wound modifiers, then cap their net change at −1/+1. Modified charge totals are at most 12. Detection/Lone Operative distances are 9–30 inches. Stratagem CP cost can increase by at most one and cannot fall below zero.

## Random characteristics — 02.02.03

- Random M: when selected to move, roll the indicated dice once for the entire unit's move distance.
- Random A: determine at Resolve Attacks/gathering, separately for each weapon, even when identical attacks will be grouped; combine the generated counts afterward.
- Random D: determine independently each time an attack inflicts damage, after the opposing player selects the recipient model. That determination is a damage roll. An operator suffix such as the `+1` in D6+1 belongs to the characteristic, rather than being a modifier.
- Other random characteristics: determine individually per model/weapon each time needed.

Current Stat fixtures represent fixed values and a single D3/D6-style random domain; sums/operators and new random model characteristics need corresponding supplied datasheets and representation in their owning tasks. The user directs us to assume random-A melee weapons do not exist. Melee target declaration asserts fixed A; unsupported data fails explicitly rather than inventing a split policy.

## Re-rolls — 01.05.02

When rerolling a roll formed by adding several dice (2D6/3D6/etc.), reroll all contributing dice. Each die may be rerolled at most once. Rerolls precede modifiers. Replacement results are still dice rolls and can trigger applicable rules.

## Target no longer eligible or viable — 04.03.03

If a previously eligible target for an attack or rule stops being eligible—for example, it moves out of range during an out-of-phase move—the controlling player can select new targets.

The task-9 implementation permits replacement targets for unresolved weapon shares, preserving their attack count and applying current ordinary target legality. Destroyed targets are nonviable. It does not redirect attacks that have already been resolved.

## Multiple Weapon Profiles — 04.01.03

Source: user-supplied appendix wording.

In the Select Weapons step (04.01), if a selected weapon has more than one profile, then the controlling player must also select one of those profiles. The selected profile is then used in the Resolve Attacks step (04.03). Some of these profiles are known as Hunter profiles. Hunter profiles can only target units with the specified keywords. Note that if a unit is equipped with more than one such weapon, a different profile can be selected for each model within that unit.


## Fight on Death — user supplied during task 14 (appendix number not supplied)

Some rules enable models to attack after they have been destroyed. When a model under such an effect is destroyed, do not remove it from play. Those models will stay on the battlefield until their unit is selected to attack and has attacked, or until the end of the phase (whichever comes first). Any rules triggered by those models being destroyed are resolved, and then those destroyed models are removed.

If a rule instructs a destroyed model to fight immediately after the attacking unit, instead that model is not removed from the battlefield until that model’s unit has fought, or until the end of the phase (whichever comes first). This allows the models in the unit to fight all at once, and stratagems that target that unit will also affect that destroyed model.

## Persisting Rules Effects — 01.02.02 (user supplied during task 16)

Some rules apply an effect that lasts until a certain duration has passed (e.g. until the start of your next turn). Such effects are known as persisting effects. If a persisting effect applies to a unit when you place it in strategic reserves or when it embarks within a TRANSPORT, make a note of that effect and its duration; if that unit is set up on the battlefield again, any persisting effects continue to apply to that unit for their full duration.

If you have a rule that specifies when it is triggered (e.g. ‘In the Shooting phase’) and it does not specify how long it is active for, it is only active for the duration of that specified period (e.g. until the end of that Shooting phase). After that period, that effect is no longer applicable/active. If a rule or effect is given to a unit and it does not specify how long it is active for, it is only active for the phase in which it was given.

## Not On The Battlefield — 01.02.04 (user supplied during task 16)

A unit that is embarked within a TRANSPORT or that is in strategic reserves is not on the battlefield. The following applies to such units:

- That unit is not visible to any other units (units are visible to themselves).
- Any other unit is not visible to that unit.
- Players cannot measure distances to or from that unit (units are within range of their own abilities).

This means units not on the battlefield cannot be selected or targeted by any attack or rule that requires a unit to be visible or within a certain distance (other than their own abilities). Such units can still use their other rules, and are still units in the controlling player’s army and so can be affected by rules that require a player to select a unit from an army, as well as rules that affect all units in an army. The controlling player must make battle-shock rolls for units that are not on the battlefield in their Command phase if they are battle-shocked, or if they are at or below half-strength.

## Ignore Modifiers — 02.02.02 (retrieved during task17)

Source: [rules appendix, Ignore Modifiers](https://wahapedia.ru/wh40k11ed/the-rules/rules-appendix/#Ignore-Modifiers), retrieved2026-10-12 alongside the Orks audit. This is an appendix definition, not a quotation from a core-PDF page.

Some rules enable a player, weapon, model or unit to ignore modifiers. When using such rules, the following points apply:

- Unless otherwise stated, that rule allows you to ignore modifiers to the unit’s rolls and the unit’s profile and weapon characteristics.
- When a rule states you can ignore modifiers to a stated roll or characteristic, you can choose to ignore all of those modifiers, or only some of them. For example, you can still choose to apply positive/beneficial modifiers while ignoring negative/detrimental modifiers.

Example: Your unit can ignore characteristic modifiers and is under the effect of +2"M and -2"M. You can choose to ignore the -2"M, meaning that unit’s M characteristic will only be modified by the +2"M.

Example: Your unit’s ranged attacks can ignore roll modifiers and are under the effect of +1 to hit rolls and -1 to hit rolls. You can choose to ignore the -1 to hit rolls, meaning those attacks will only be affected by the +1 to hit rolls.

Implementation boundary:17.C4b integrates explicit WS/BS and hit-roll permissions. The definition’s unqualified coverage of every characteristic/roll does not imply those additional consumers have been implemented. Forge World/Legends units are excluded; no Gargantuan Squiggoth grant is installed.
