IMPLEMENTATION READING CONTRACT

This reference has three parts:
  PART I — procedural navigation. The numbered A-W entries explain the flow in natural language. They are editorial restatements, not replacement rule wording.
  PART II — retained source wording. Use this part to decide exact eligibility, obligations, choices, timing, distance conditions and exceptions. Every excerpt carries a source-page citation and a stable Pxx-yy locator. Original rule reference numbers remain searchable.
  PART III — implementation uncertainties and source dependencies. These must remain explicit until the relevant rule or clarification is available.

For a future edition migration, use PART I to find the procedure, then verify every implemented condition against PART II. Where the guide and the source wording differ, use the source wording and correct the guide. Do not resolve an ambiguity by importing an older-edition rule, assuming a familiar mechanic, or rewriting the source into a stronger requirement.

Text-conversion conventions:
  - Physical line wrapping and columns have been removed; the source's operative words, negatives, quantities and modal verbs are retained.
  - Bullet and arrow shapes express list structure, not game mechanics. Tables are converted to explicitly labelled rows; dice icons are written as their numeric results.
  - Page references apply to all subordinate statements in their cited entry. Pxx-yy locators are editorial and are not original book reference numbers.
  - Examples, designer's notes, explanatory sidebars, and FAQs are retained. They remain those kinds of material; an example is not an additional universal rule.
  - Printed cross-reference lists are retained to expose dependencies. A reference to app content does not reproduce that content.
  - Directional phrases such as 'see opposite' remain source wording. They refer to the original PDF's layout: consult the adjacent source-page excerpts or the explicitly numbered referenced rule, rather than this text file's physical position.
  - Running section titles, decorative slogans, page furniture, isolated figure tokens and artwork are omitted. The operative captions for figures are retained. The tilted sample stratagem card on p. 55 is covered by the identical complete upright rules on p. 57.
  - The Boyz example, attack tables, attached-unit ability table, unit-strength table, and graphical dice needed for the attack examples were reconstructed as text and checked against rendered PDF pages. Layout changes are not presented as additional official wording.

This file faithfully preserves the uploaded source where rules are available; it cannot make an incomplete or ambiguous source unambiguous by itself. PART III records concrete cases requiring care. This is a reference for the supplied PDF, not a claim that external rules, later updates, or every rare interaction have been supplied.

A. PREPARING A BATTLE AND CORE CONCEPTS

A1. Establish the mission and army rules.
Before playing, generate a mission, for example using a mission deck. Use that mission's instructions for battlefield preparation, deployment, additional rules and victory conditions. The mission also specifies the size of the armies to muster. Obtain the applicable faction/datasheet rules for those armies. Battles normally last five battle rounds, but use the mission's stated duration rather than assuming five rounds in every case. The uploaded core book does not itself provide a complete mission or the army-mustering procedure. [Source: pp. 4-5.]

A2. Identify each army and its controller — 01.01.
Each player commands an army of units of models. A player controls every model in their own army. When a rule refers to a controlling player, use the player who controls the affected models. [Source: p. 8.]

A3. Identify units, models, friendly and enemy — 01.02.
A unit consists of one or more models that move and fight together. A model's base, if present, is part of that model for rules purposes. Friendly models and units are in your army; enemy models and units are in your opponent's army. If a rule does not specify friendly or enemy, it can affect either army. An effect or ability that applies to a unit applies to every model in that unit. [Source: p. 8.]

A4. Determine the active and opposing players — 01.03.
The two players always have opposite roles: one active, the other opposing. Normally the player whose turn it is is active. When neither player is taking a turn, such as at the start or end of a battle round, the player who takes the first turn in each round is active. Temporarily change these roles in either of these situations:
  1. When a unit is selected to move, its controller is active until the move ends.
  2. When a unit is selected to shoot or fight, its controller is active until those attacks are resolved.
Whenever one player becomes active, the other becomes opposing. [Source: p. 8.]

A5. Measure distances — 01.04.
Use inches. Players can measure distances whenever they wish. Unless a rule says otherwise, determine a model's position relative to something else by measuring to or from the closest part of its base. Apply the FRAME exception for models with that keyword; see P2. [Source: pp. 9, 62.]

A6. Interpret dice instructions — 01.05.
Use six-sided dice, abbreviated D6. A result written as X+ means X or higher. A range such as 1-3 includes each listed result. For 2D6, 3D6, and so on, roll that many D6 and add their values. For D3, roll one D6, halve its value and round up. For D6+1, 2D6+3, and similar instructions, roll the stated dice, add their values, then add the stated constant. [Source: p. 9.]

A7. Make a leadership roll — 01.06.
The unit's controller rolls 2D6. The roll succeeds if its result equals or exceeds at least one Leadership characteristic in the unit. Otherwise it fails. Apply the success or failure effect specified by the rule requiring the roll. [Source: p. 9.]

A8. Make a battle-shock roll and apply battle-shock — 01.07.
Make a leadership roll for the unit. A success does not make the unit battle-shocked; a failure makes the unit and every model in it battle-shocked. While battle-shocked:
  1. Every model's Objective Control characteristic is modified to '-'.
  2. The controller cannot target the unit with stratagems.
  3. The unit cannot start an action, and an action it has started cannot be completed.
The Command phase provides an explicit recovery procedure for already battle-shocked units; a successful roll elsewhere does not, merely from this general rule, establish recovery. [Source: pp. 9, 30.]

B. DATASHEETS

B1. Read the model profile — 02.01-02.02.
Identify the unit by its datasheet name. Read each relevant model's characteristics:
  M, Move: its movement speed. A model with M '-' can be set up but otherwise cannot be moved.
  T, Toughness: its resistance to being wounded.
  Sv, Save: the armour-save threshold, such as 4+.
  InSv, Invulnerable Save: an additional save threshold, if present.
  W, Wounds: how much damage the model can sustain. Remaining wounds of zero or less destroy it.
  Ld, Leadership: the threshold used for leadership rolls, such as 7+.
  OC, Objective Control: its contribution to controlling objectives. A model with OC '-' cannot control objectives at all.
Use the profile appropriate to the particular model; not every model in a unit necessarily has the same profile. [Source: pp. 10-11.]

B2. Read abilities and weapon profiles — 02.03-02.04.
Apply the unit's listed abilities where their conditions are met. For each weapon, read:
  R, Range: the distance a ranged weapon can shoot; 'Melee' identifies a melee weapon.
  A, Attacks: the number of attack dice gathered when that weapon is used.
  BS, Ballistic Skill: the hit threshold for ranged attacks.
  WS, Weapon Skill: the hit threshold for melee attacks.
  S, Strength: the value compared with target Toughness when rolling to wound.
  AP, Armour Penetration: the modifier applied to the armour-save roll.
  D, Damage: the wounds lost when an attack inflicts damage.
Weapon abilities can modify these procedures; see section V. [Source: p. 10.]

B3. Apply keywords — 02.05.
Datasheets list faction keywords and other keywords. Faction keywords are used when selecting an army; otherwise both types function in the same way. A rule restricted to a keyword applies only to units or models that have that keyword, according to the wording of that rule. Singular and plural versions of a keyword are equivalent. Do not give an individual model all of its unit's keywords merely because the unit has mixed keywords. [Source: pp. 10-11, 67, 87.]

B4. Apply composition and wargear options — 02.06-02.07.
Use the datasheet's unit composition to identify the permitted number and types of models and their default wargear. Follow any other listed rules, such as permitted leader attachments or transport passengers. When including the unit in an army, use its listed wargear options to change equipment where allowed. [Source: p. 11.]

C. MOVING, SETTING UP, COHERENCY AND ENGAGEMENT

C1. Resolve a move using its own conditions — 03.01.
For the selected move type, check eligibility, its maximum movement distance or setup distance, and all before-moving, while-moving and after-moving requirements. Move one or more models, one at a time. Each model may move along multiple straight segments and rotate multiple times. Unless a specific rule permits otherwise:
  1. It may move through friendly models.
  2. Its base may pass through a space large enough for that base.
  3. Its base may not pass through enemy models.
  4. Its base may not cross the battlefield edge.
  5. Every while-moving condition of the move type must be met.
Terrain and special model rules can change what the model can pass through. [Source: p. 12.]

C2. Measure movement and rotation — 03.01.
For a straight movement segment, move horizontally and measure from the same point on the base before and after the segment. Add that distance to all previous movement distance for that model during this unit's move. Do not exceed the move's maximum distance. Rotate a model upright around the centre of its base; rotation does not consume movement distance. For a baseless FRAME model, rotate upright around its central axis. Where vertical movement is required, add ascent and descent as described in L3. [Source: pp. 12-13, 48, 62.]

C3. Check whether a move can finish — 03.01.
After setting up all models and/or moving the models you wish to move, check:
  1. If on the battlefield, the unit is in coherency.
  2. No model is on another model or partway through a terrain surface such as a wall or ceiling.
  3. All after-moving conditions are satisfied.
If any check fails, the unit cannot make the move; return its models to their starting positions. Otherwise resolve any additional after-moving rules and end the move. [Source: p. 12.]

C4. Set up a unit — 03.02.
Place every model so that the unit is in coherency, is unengaged, and satisfies every other setup requirement and restriction. If you cannot place all models, remove the unit from the battlefield and return it to its original location, such as strategic reserves or inside its transport. When a failed setup was attempted as a move, the unit has not been selected to make that move: it may be selected later for another setup attempt or to remain stationary. Specific exceptions, such as combat or emergency disembarkation, must be applied as stated in those procedures. [Source: p. 14; exceptions on p. 65.]

C5. Maintain coherency — 03.03.
A unit with more than one model must be set up and finish every type of move in coherency. For every model in that unit, both must be true:
  1. It is within 2 inches horizontally and 5 inches vertically of at least one other model in the unit.
  2. It is within 9 inches horizontally and 5 inches vertically of every other model in the unit.
At each player's End of Turn step, controllers of any incoherent units on the battlefield remove models from those units one at a time until coherency is restored. Those models are destroyed, but do not trigger rules for a model being destroyed. [Source: pp. 14-15.]

C6. Determine engagement — 03.04.
A model's engagement range extends 2 inches horizontally and 5 inches vertically. If a friendly model is within engagement range of an enemy model, those models and their units are engaged with each other. A unit is unengaged if it contains no engaged models. [Source: pp. 14-15.]

D. SELECTING WEAPONS AND RESOLVING ATTACKS

D1. Start a shooting or fighting activation — 04.01.
When a unit shoots or fights, the active player first selects weapons, then selects their targets, then resolves the attacks. For each attacking model:
  When shooting, select one or more of its ranged weapons, subject to shooting-type and weapon restrictions.
  When fighting, select one of its melee weapons; EXTRA ATTACKS modifies this selection.
A model with no ranged weapons cannot make ranged attacks; a model with no melee weapons cannot make melee attacks. [Source: p. 16; weapon exceptions on pp. 80-81.]

D2. Select targets for ranged weapons — 04.02.
For each selected ranged weapon, select one enemy unit. Unless another rule says otherwise, that unit must be visible to the firing model, within that weapon's range, and unengaged. Different weapons can target different units. If no legal target exists for a weapon, or you choose not to select a target for a ranged weapon, the model makes no attacks with that weapon. [Source: p. 16.]

D3. Select targets and split melee attacks — 04.02-04.03.
For each selected melee weapon, select one or more enemy units engaged with its bearer. Do not select more target units than the weapon's A characteristic. If selecting multiple targets, declare how many attacks that weapon will make against each: allocate at least one attack to each target. When gathering dice against a particular target, gather only the number declared against that target. Different weapons can select different targets. [Source: pp. 16-17.]

D4. Group and resolve weapons against targets — 04.03.
  1. Select an enemy unit targeted by one or more unresolved weapons.
  2. Select one unresolved weapon targeting that unit. Gather D6 equal to its A characteristic, or its declared share of split melee attacks.
  3. Also gather dice for every other unresolved weapon targeting that unit that makes identical attacks. Attacks are identical only if BS/WS, S, AP and D match and the same applicable abilities and rules affect them.
  4. Resolve the attack sequence for all dice in this group.
  5. If another weapon targeting the same unit remains unresolved, repeat from step 2.
  6. Otherwise, if attacks against another target remain unresolved, repeat from step 1.
  7. Otherwise the attacking unit's attacks are resolved.
Apply effects that add attack dice when gathering them. [Source: p. 17.]

D5. General attack sequence — 05.
Resolve hit rolls, then wound rolls, then save rolls, then damage. Within a step, roll all required dice simultaneously if there is more than one. An individual attack's sequence ends when it fails or inflicts damage. The group ends when all its attacks have failed or inflicted damage. Critical hits are hits and critical wounds are wounds; they may also trigger abilities. [Source: p. 18.]

D6. Roll to hit — 05.01.
For every attack die, roll one D6. Check these conditions in this order and apply the first that matches:
  1. An unmodified 1 fails.
  2. An unmodified 6 is a critical hit.
  3. A result equal to or higher than that attack's BS for shooting, or WS for fighting, is a hit.
  4. Any other result fails.
Specific shooting types or abilities can replace this procedure. The dice symbols for 1 and 6 were checked directly on the PDF page. [Source: p. 18.]

D7. Roll to wound — 05.02.
For every hit requiring a wound roll, roll one D6. Check these conditions in order:
  1. An unmodified 1 fails.
  2. An unmodified 6 is a critical wound.
  3. Otherwise the attack wounds if the result meets the following threshold, comparing Strength S with target Toughness T:
       S is at least twice T: 2+.
       S is greater than T but less than twice T: 3+.
       S equals T: 4+.
       S is less than T but greater than half T: 5+.
       S is at most half T: 6+.
  4. Any other result fails.
Abilities such as ANTI can change critical-wound thresholds. For attached-unit Toughness use R2. The numeric thresholds were checked visually because the PDF represents them with dice symbols. [Source: pp. 18, 66, 79.]

D8. Create allocation groups and declare their order — 05.03.
The opposing player divides the target unit's models into allocation groups:
  Create a separate group for each CHARACTER model.
  Group other models together if they have the same W, Sv and InSv characteristics.
The opposing player then declares the groups' allocation order, obeying all of these restrictions:
  1. A non-CHARACTER group containing a model with lost wounds must be first.
  2. All non-CHARACTER groups precede all CHARACTER groups.
  3. Wounded CHARACTER groups precede unwounded CHARACTER groups.
The first group is the current group. When every model in it is destroyed, advance to the next group. PRECISION can override the current group as described in V28. [Source: pp. 19, 84.]

D9. Roll saves — 05.03.
The opposing player rolls one D6 for every attack that wounded the target. These rolls are made before damage is processed through the allocation groups. [Source: p. 19.]

D10. Process saves from lowest to highest and inflict damage — 05.04.
For each save result, in ascending order, do the following until all attacks are resolved or every target model is destroyed:
  1. Select a model from the current allocation group. If a model in that group has lost wounds, select a wounded model if possible.
  2. Check the save using the first matching condition:
       An unmodified 1 causes the attack to inflict damage.
       Otherwise, if the group has an InSv characteristic and the result meets or exceeds it, the attack fails.
       Otherwise, modify the result by the weapon's AP; if it then meets or exceeds the group's Sv characteristic, the attack fails.
       Otherwise the attack inflicts damage.
  3. If the attack inflicts damage, the selected model loses wounds equal to that attack's D. At zero or fewer remaining wounds, it is destroyed.
  4. If that destroys the last model in the current allocation group, the next group becomes current before the next save is processed.
If every target model is destroyed, any excess attacks are lost. This is a single save-result procedure testing InSv and Sv in sequence, not a choice to make separate invulnerable and armour-save rolls. AP applies at the Sv check, not the InSv check. [Source: p. 19.]

D11. Allocation example: characteristics can change as groups are destroyed.
The illustrated attached unit has a non-CHARACTER Geminae group first, a non-CHARACTER Seraphim group second, and Saint Celestine's CHARACTER group last. The lowest two save results destroy the Geminae. The next result is then checked against the Seraphim's characteristics, because that group is now current. This illustrates why the allocation order must be declared before resolving damage and why saves are processed in ascending order. [Source: pp. 22-23; example, not an additional rule.]

E. VISIBILITY, MORTAL WOUNDS AND HAZARDS

E1. Check line of sight — 06.01.
To see a model, the observing model must be able to draw an imaginary straight line 1 millimetre wide from any part of itself to any part of the observed model. Ignore other models in the observing model's unit and the observed model's unit while checking. Apply terrain visibility rules in addition to this basic test. [Source: p. 24.]

E2. Distinguish visible from fully visible — 06.01.
A model is visible if any part is visible. A model is fully visible if every part facing the observer is visible, so only the observed model itself blocks sight of its own remaining parts. A unit is visible if at least one of its models is visible. A unit is fully visible if every one of its models is fully visible; the observer can see through other models in that unit for this check. [Source: p. 25.]

E3. Allocate mortal wounds — 06.02.
For each mortal wound suffered by a unit, repeat this procedure until all are inflicted or the unit is destroyed:
  1. If a non-CHARACTER model has lost wounds, select it.
  2. Otherwise, if a non-CHARACTER model remains, select one.
  3. Otherwise, if a CHARACTER model has lost wounds, select one such model.
  4. Otherwise select one CHARACTER model.
  5. The selected model loses one wound and is destroyed if this reduces it to zero remaining wounds.
This sequence does not contain a save roll. Abilities such as Feel No Pain may prevent a wound being lost. DEVASTATING WOUNDS has its own per-attack limit on how many models can be damaged. [Source: pp. 24, 80-81.]

E4. Resolve mixed normal and mortal damage — 06.02.
If a group of attack dice inflicts both normal damage and mortal wounds, resolve all normal damage from those attacks first, then their mortal wounds. [Source: p. 24.]

E5. Make hazard rolls — 06.03.
Roll one D6 per required hazard roll. On 1-2 the roll fails: the unit suffers one mortal wound, or three mortal wounds instead if every model in it is a MONSTER/VEHICLE model. Roll multiple required hazard dice simultaneously. Resolve the resulting mortal wounds using E3. [Source: p. 24.]

F. BATTLE ROUNDS AND TURNS

F1. Resolve each battle round — 07.01-07.03.
  1. Resolve rules triggered at the start of the battle round.
  2. Both players take a turn. The mission determines who takes the first turn, and that same player goes first in every round.
  3. At the end of the round, resolve non-mission end-of-round rules first. Then each player checks the mission and resolves mission achievements triggered at that time.
  4. End the round. If the mission does not end the battle, begin the next round.
Use the mission's required number of battle rounds. [Source: p. 28.]

F2. Resolve a turn in order — 07.02.
  1. Start of Turn step: resolve start-of-turn rules.
  2. Command phase.
  3. Movement phase.
  4. Shooting phase.
  5. Charge phase.
  6. Fight phase.
  7. End of Turn step: resolve non-mission end-of-turn rules first, then both players resolve mission achievements triggered at that time.
Also restore coherency at the End of Turn step as in C5. [Source: pp. 14, 28-29.]

G. COMMAND PHASE

G1. Resolve the Command phase — 08.01-08.05.
  1. Resolve rules triggered at the start of the Command phase.
  2. Gain Core CP: both players gain one Command Point. These are ordinary CP, named Core CP to identify this source.
  3. Battle-shock: the active player rolls once for each friendly unit that is currently battle-shocked and/or at or below half-strength. If it was battle-shocked at the start of this step and succeeds on its roll in this step, it is no longer battle-shocked. Failures apply battle-shock as in A8.
  4. Resolve other Command-phase abilities, excluding rules already assigned to start/end of phase, gaining Core CP, or battle-shock rolls.
  5. At the end of the phase, resolve non-mission end-of-phase rules first, then both players resolve mission achievements triggered at that time.
Do not treat battle-shock as automatically expiring at the start of the turn: use the stated recovery roll. Determine half-strength using W1. [Source: pp. 30-31, 86.]

H. MOVEMENT PHASE

H1. Resolve the Movement phase — 09.01-09.03.
  1. Resolve start-of-Movement-phase rules.
  2. Select and move friendly units one at a time. Select a unit not yet selected to move this phase, including units on the battlefield, in strategic reserves, or embarked in a transport. Select a move type it is eligible to make and resolve it fully before proceeding.
  3. Continue until every friendly unit has been selected to move, even if its selected type is remain stationary.
  4. Resolve end-of-Movement-phase rules.
The listed move types are remain stationary, normal, advance, fall-back, disembark and ingress; other rules can provide additional types. [Source: p. 32.]

H2. Remain stationary — 09.04.
Any unit is eligible. Move no model, including by rotation. A unit remaining stationary does not trigger rules for starting or ending a move. [Source: p. 33.]

H3. Make a normal move — 09.05.
Eligible unit: on the battlefield and unengaged. Maximum distance: its M characteristic, using each model's own M where they differ. Move under section C and finish unengaged. [Source: pp. 33, 87.]

H4. Make an advance move — 09.06.
Eligible unit: on the battlefield and unengaged. Before moving, roll one D6 for the advance roll. Maximum distance: M plus that roll, using each model's own M where they differ. Move under section C and finish unengaged. Until the turn ends, the unit cannot declare a charge or start an action unless another rule says otherwise. Its shooting eligibility is governed by the shooting types; ASSAULT enables the specified advance-and-shoot procedure. [Source: pp. 33, 35, 87.]

H5. Make a fall-back move — 09.07.
Eligible unit: engaged. Maximum distance: M. Before moving choose a mode:
  Ordered Retreat: available if the unit is not battle-shocked; this mode is optional even when available.
  Desperate Escape: required if Ordered Retreat is not chosen. Before moving, make one hazard roll per model in the unit. Models that move can pass through enemy models in this mode.
Move under section C and finish unengaged. Until the turn ends, the unit cannot shoot, declare a charge or start an action unless otherwise stated. If using Desperate Escape and the unit is not battle-shocked after moving, make a battle-shock roll for it. [Source: p. 33.]

H6. Apply movement modes in their stated order.
Modes are mutually exclusive. Assess them in the order printed for that move. Some are mandatory when their conditions hold, such as consolidation modes. Others are optional, such as Ordered Retreat. If no mode's conditions are satisfied, the move cannot be made. Requirements labelled with a mode apply only to that mode; unlabelled requirements apply to every mode. [Source: p. 33.]

I. SHOOTING PHASE

I1. Resolve the Shooting phase — 10.01-10.03.
  1. Resolve start-of-Shooting-phase rules.
  2. Select friendly units you choose to shoot with, one at a time. A unit is eligible to shoot if on the battlefield and not already selected to shoot this phase, subject to other restrictions such as fall-back or starting an action.
  3. For each selected unit, choose a shooting type it is eligible to use and resolve its attacks fully.
  4. Resolve end-of-Shooting-phase rules.
Units with no ranged weapons can still be eligible to shoot, although they cannot make ranged attacks. [Source: pp. 33-35, 58, 88.]

I2. Use normal shooting — 10.04.
Eligible unit: unengaged and did not advance this turn. Resolve shooting under section D. After shooting, it cannot start an action for the remainder of this phase. [Source: p. 34.]

I3. Use assault shooting — 10.05.
Eligible unit: unengaged, advanced this turn, and has at least one ASSAULT weapon. Resolve shooting under section D but select only ASSAULT weapons. After shooting, it cannot start an action for the remainder of this phase. [Source: p. 35.]

I4. Use close-quarters shooting — 10.06.
Eligible unit: engaged, did not advance this turn, and has at least one CLOSE-QUARTERS weapon or is a MONSTER/VEHICLE unit. Resolve shooting under section D with these changes:
  1. Models may target enemy units their unit is engaged with.
  2. For MONSTER/VEHICLE models, subtract one from each attack's hit roll unless the attack uses a CLOSE-QUARTERS weapon against a unit engaged with their unit. Their BLAST weapons still cannot target a unit engaged with their unit.
  3. Other models may select only CLOSE-QUARTERS weapons and may target only enemy units engaged with their unit.
  4. After shooting, the unit cannot start an action for the remainder of the phase.
Apply the separate rules for shooting at engaged MONSTER/VEHICLE targets and the BLAST FAQ; see P3 and W9. [Source: pp. 35, 62-63, 88.]

I5. Use indirect shooting — 10.07.
Eligible unit: unengaged, did not advance this turn, and has at least one INDIRECT FIRE weapon. Resolve shooting under section D with these changes:
  1. Its INDIRECT FIRE weapons may target units not visible to their bearer. Other weapons still require their normal eligible targets.
  2. Every attack with an INDIRECT FIRE weapon gives its target the benefit of cover.
  3. Do not re-roll hit rolls for those INDIRECT FIRE attacks.
  4. Those attacks fail on unmodified hit rolls of 1-5. Instead, they fail on unmodified 1-3 if the firing unit remained stationary this turn and the target is visible to at least one friendly unit.
  5. After shooting, the firing unit cannot start an action for the remainder of the phase.
These INDIRECT FIRE attack restrictions are not stated to depend on whether that particular target is visible. A qualifying result not forced to fail still follows the applicable attack rules. [Source: p. 35.]

I6. Use snap shooting — 15.09.
Eligibility comes from the rule granting this shooting type. Resolve shooting under section D, but target only one visible enemy unit within 24 inches of the shooting unit, and only if otherwise an eligible target. Every attack hits only on an unmodified 6, irrespective of BS or modifiers. Hit rolls cannot be re-rolled. After shooting, the unit cannot start an action for the remainder of the phase. FIRE OVERWATCH grants this shooting type. [Source: p. 57.]

J. CHARGE PHASE

J1. Resolve the Charge phase — 11.01-11.03.
  1. Resolve start-of-Charge-phase rules.
  2. Select a friendly unit not yet declared to charge this phase and eligible to declare a charge.
  3. Roll 2D6 for its charge roll; the result is its charge move's maximum distance.
  4. If a legal charge move is possible and you still want to make it, make it. Otherwise the unit does not make a charge move. Either way, the declared charge is resolved.
  5. Repeat with other units you choose to charge with.
  6. Resolve end-of-Charge-phase rules.
Unless otherwise stated, a unit must be on the battlefield to declare a charge. It is prevented from declaring if not within 12 inches of any enemy unit, if engaged, or if it advanced or fell back this turn. Other rules can impose further restrictions. [Source: p. 36.]

J2. Choose charge targets after rolling and make the charge move — 11.04.
Eligible unit: declared a charge this phase. Maximum distance: its charge roll, subject to any applicable adjustment such as taking to the skies. Before moving, select one or more enemy units that are both within 12 inches of the charging unit and within its maximum movement distance. They are the charge targets until this move ends.
During the move:
  1. Every model must finish closer to at least one charge target.
  2. Every model that can finish within 1 inch of at least one charge target must do so.
  3. Every model that can finish engaged with at least one charge target must do so.
After moving:
  1. The unit must be engaged with every charge target.
  2. It must not be engaged with any enemy unit that was not a charge target.
  3. Every model in the charging unit gains Fights First until the turn ends.
Also meet the general movement completion conditions. [Source: p. 37.]

J3. Charge-distance examples.
Without modifiers, a charge roll of two cannot complete a charge: the unit had to be outside the 2-inch engagement range to declare, and charge targets must be within the maximum distance. A target beyond the charge-roll distance cannot be chosen merely because moving that distance would get within 2 inches of it. Even a target within the roll's distance can be unreachable if intervening terrain prevents a legal route. [Source: pp. 36-37; explanatory examples.]

K. FIGHT PHASE

K1. Resolve the Fight phase in five distinct steps — 12.01-12.09.
  1. Start of Fight phase: resolve start-of-phase rules.
  2. Pile In: the player whose turn it is makes all chosen eligible pile-in moves first, then the opponent does so.
  3. Fight: resolve all eligible units' fights using K4.
  4. Consolidate: the player whose turn it is makes all chosen eligible consolidation moves first, then the opponent does so.
  5. End of Fight phase: resolve end-of-phase rules.
All units that can fight must fight, subject to the appendix passing procedure. Pile-in and consolidation moves are optional. Do not merge these separate phase-wide movement steps into each unit's normal fight activation. [Source: pp. 38, 40, 42, 87.]

K2. Make a pile-in move — 12.02-12.03.
Maximum distance: 3 inches. During the Fight phase, a unit is eligible if it is engaged, made a charge move this turn, or was selected for an overrun fight this phase. Each unit may pile in at most once during the Pile In step; an overrun fight separately permits an additional pile-in.
Before moving, if the unit is engaged, select every enemy unit it is engaged with as pile-in targets. Otherwise select one or more enemy units within 5 inches.
During the move:
  1. Models in base-contact with enemy models cannot move.
  2. Each model that moves must finish closer to the closest pile-in target, and engaged with it if possible.
After moving, the unit must be engaged. Each model that began engaged with an enemy unit must still be engaged with that enemy unit. Apply general movement conditions too. [Source: pp. 38, 40.]

K3. Determine fight eligibility — 12.04.
A unit is eligible to fight if not already selected to fight this phase and at least one applies:
  1. It is engaged, or was engaged at the start of the Fight step.
  2. It made a charge move this turn.
Check this eligibility independently of whether a particular fight type is available. [Source: p. 40.]

K4. Alternate fight selections, prioritising Fights First — 12.04.
First resolve Fights First combats. Starting with the player whose turn it is, alternate selecting one friendly eligible Fights First unit, then resolve its chosen fight type. If a player cannot select such a unit:
  If no eligible Fights First units remain for either player, change to Remaining Combats; this player selects their next unit there.
  Otherwise the other player makes the next Fights First selection.
In Remaining Combats, alternate selecting one friendly eligible unit, starting with the player who moved the sequence into this step. If a player cannot select:
  If no eligible units remain, end the Fight step.
  Otherwise the other player selects next.
After resolving a fight in Remaining Combats, if at least one Fights First unit has become eligible, return to Fights First Combats. Continue until all eligible fights are resolved, applying the appendix pass rule when appropriate. [Source: pp. 40, 87.]

K5. Select the fight type — 12.05-12.06.
When selecting an eligible unit to fight, choose a fight type it is eligible to make.
  Normal fight: the unit is engaged; resolve melee attacks using section D.
  Overrun fight: the unit is unengaged, or it was unengaged at the start of the Fight step but became engaged during the Fight phase. It may make one additional pile-in move, then resolves melee attacks using section D.
An overrun may engage a previously unengaged enemy, making that enemy eligible to fight; a Fights First enemy may then enter the priority sequence. The overrun condition alone does not confer eligibility to be selected to fight. [Source: pp. 40-41, 88.]

K6. Choose consolidation modes — 12.07-12.08.
During the Consolidate step, each unit that was eligible to fight this phase may make at most one consolidation move. Maximum distance: 3 inches. Before moving, select the first applicable mode; the choice is mandatory in this order:
  1. Ongoing Consolidation: if engaged, select every enemy unit the unit is engaged with.
  2. Engaging Consolidation: otherwise, if within 3 inches of one or more enemy units, select one or more such enemy units.
  3. Objective Consolidation: otherwise, if within 3 inches of one or more objectives, select one such objective.
If no mode is available, the move cannot be made. [Source: pp. 33, 42.]

K7. Make an Ongoing Consolidation move — 12.08.
Do not move models in base-contact with enemy models. Each model that moves must finish closer to the closest selected enemy unit and engaged with it if possible. Each model that started engaged with an enemy unit must remain engaged with that enemy unit after moving. Meet general movement conditions. [Source: p. 42.]

K8. Make an Engaging Consolidation move — 12.08.
Each model that moves must finish closer to the closest selected enemy unit, and engaged with it if possible. The unit must finish engaged with every selected enemy unit. If any enemy unit engaged with the consolidating unit has not yet been selected to fight this phase, the opponent must select each such enemy unit one at a time; each becomes eligible and is selected to fight under the Fight procedure. Meet general movement conditions. [Source: p. 42.]

K9. Make an Objective Consolidation move — 12.08.
Each model that moves must finish within range of the selected objective if possible, or closer to that objective otherwise. The unit must finish within range of the selected objective. Meet general movement conditions. [Source: pp. 42-43.]

L. TERRAIN

L1. Establish terrain areas before the battle — 13.01.
Create each terrain area by one of these methods:
  1. Place a clearly bounded base or mat, then place one or more terrain features wholly inside it.
  2. Place one terrain feature directly on the battlefield; its occupied area is the terrain area.
  3. Place several terrain features directly on the battlefield so that together they define an area's boundary.
Use the mission's terrain-area locations and dimensions if supplied; otherwise agree them with the opponent before the battle. A terrain area can contain features from different categories. If a mission gives no terrain guidance, players decide the battlefield layout. Leave room for large models to manoeuvre, particularly near edges; the book advises that too little dense terrain can favour shooting and disadvantage melee. [Source: pp. 46-47.]

L2. Assign a category to each terrain feature — 13.02-13.05.
  Exposed: examples include craters, razorwire and scattered debris.
  Light: examples include barricades, low walls and statuary.
  Dense: examples include buildings, ruins, armoured containers and woods.
A mission can specify which categories each area should contain. Apply movement and visibility rules to the categories actually present; the area and its individual features are distinct things. [Source: pp. 46-47.]

L3. Move through and over terrain — 13.06.
  1. All models may move horizontally and vertically through exposed or light features.
  2. INFANTRY, BEASTS, SWARM and MOBILE models may move horizontally through dense features.
  3. INFANTRY, BEASTS and SWARM models may move vertically through dense features.
  4. Other models may move horizontally through dense features only if every section their base would pass through is no more than 2 inches high. For taller sections they must ascend and descend vertically instead. They cannot pass through ceilings or floors while doing so, and cannot finish on a surface above ground level under this permission.
For vertical movement, keep the model within half an inch horizontally of the feature and count both upward and downward distance toward the move's distance. Moving through the open space of a terrain area does not itself require moving through its terrain features. Flying and other special rules can modify this procedure. [Source: pp. 48-49.]

L4. Set up or finish a move on terrain — 13.06.
Models may be set up or finish on a feature's ground level. To be set up or finish on a surface above ground level, a model must have at least one of INFANTRY, BEASTS, SWARM, FLY or MONSTER, must be stable, and must not overhang the surface's outer edge with any part of its base. Also meet applicable setup/movement restrictions. Dense terrain's Solid rule does not allow a model to finish projecting through low enclosed parts, including small openings such as doors or windows, to circumvent visibility restrictions. [Source: p. 48.]

L5. Determine the benefit of cover — 13.07-13.08.
For a ranged attack, check every model in the target unit. The unit has cover only if each model meets at least one of these conditions:
  1. It is INFANTRY, BEASTS or SWARM and is within a terrain area.
  2. It is not fully visible to the attacking model because of intervening terrain features and/or intervening obscuring terrain areas.
When the unit has cover against the attack, worsen that attack's BS characteristic by one. This is a change to BS, not a bonus to its save or a hit-roll modifier. Some other rules grant cover without these terrain conditions. [Source: pp. 50-51.]

L6. Determine whether a model is hidden — 13.09.
A model is hidden if it is INFANTRY, BEASTS or SWARM, is within a terrain area containing at least one dense feature, and its unit made no ranged attacks either this turn or the previous turn. A hidden model can be visible only to enemy models within its detection range. Detection range is 15 inches unless another rule says otherwise. Being within detection range does not override other line-of-sight restrictions. [Source: pp. 50-51.]

L7. Apply obscuring terrain areas — 13.10.
An area containing at least one light or dense feature is obscuring. When checking two models' visibility, exclude any obscuring areas that either model is within. If every line of sight between them crosses at least one remaining obscuring area, the models are not visible to each other. [Source: pp. 50-51.]

L8. Apply Solid terrain — 13.11.
Dense features have Solid. Line of sight cannot pass through an enclosed gap in the surface of such a feature that is 3 inches or less above ground level. This includes low doors, windows, bullet holes and enclosed gaps between adjacent features. A mission may adjust the height threshold. Above that threshold, the enclosed-gap prohibition does not apply, although other visibility rules still do. [Source: pp. 50-51.]

M. OBJECTIVES

M1. Identify terrain objectives — 14.01.
Use the mission to locate objectives. Normally each objective point coincides with a terrain area; that entire area becomes the terrain objective. Measure distances to and from an objective using its closest part. A model is within range of a terrain objective if within its terrain area. If the point does not coincide with an area, use the marker procedure in W6. [Source: pp. 52, 88.]

M2. Determine control — 14.02.
No objectives are controlled at the start of the battle. To gain control, a player needs one or more models with OC at least one within range. At the end of each phase and turn, calculate each player's control level by adding the OC characteristics of all their models within range:
  1. The player with the higher total controls the objective.
  2. Equal totals leave it uncontrolled unless it is secured under M3.
A friendly unit is itself controlling an objective if the player controls it, that unit is within range, and it contains at least one model with OC at least one. Models with OC '-' cannot control objectives; do not count them as positive contributors. [Source: pp. 10, 52-53.]

M3. Maintain a secured objective — 14.03.
Some rules secure an objective for a player's army. It remains under that player's control even if they leave no units in range. This lasts until, at the end of a phase, the opponent's control level is greater than theirs. A tie does not remove secured control. [Source: p. 52.]

N. STRATAGEMS

N1. Use a stratagem — 15.01.
Check its CP cost, WHEN timing, TARGET eligibility, EFFECT and RESTRICTIONS. Both players may use stratagems. Unless a rule grants an exception:
  1. A player cannot use the same stratagem more than once in a phase.
  2. A player cannot target the same unit with more than one stratagem in a phase.
To resolve one, select legal targets, pay its stated CP cost, then resolve the effect. If selecting an optional section with an additional CP cost, increase the payment by that amount. If you lack enough CP for the chosen use, you cannot use it. A battle-shocked unit cannot be targeted by its controller's stratagems under A8. INSANE BRAVERY has the pre-roll timing stated in N4; its text does not expressly override that targeting restriction. All players can use the core stratagems below; other publications can provide more. [Source: pp. 9, 54-55.]

N2. COMMAND RE-ROLL — 15.02; cost 1 CP.
WHEN: Any phase, immediately after you make an advance, charge, damage, hazard, hit, save or wound roll for a friendly model/unit, or a roll determining a weapon's number of attacks.
TARGET: The model or unit concerned.
EFFECT: Re-roll that roll. If multiple dice are being rolled together, select one die to re-roll, except that a charge roll must be re-rolled in full. This list does not include leadership or battle-shock rolls. [Source: p. 56.]

N3. EPIC CHALLENGE — 15.03; cost 1 CP.
WHEN: Fight phase, immediately after a friendly CHARACTER unit is selected to fight.
TARGET: That CHARACTER unit.
EFFECT: Select one CHARACTER model in it. Its melee weapons gain PRECISION until the phase ends. [Source: p. 56.]

N4. INSANE BRAVERY — 15.04; cost 1 CP.
WHEN: Your Command phase's Battle-shock step, immediately before making a battle-shock roll for a friendly unit.
TARGET: That unit.
EFFECT: The roll is automatically successful.
RESTRICTION: Use this stratagem no more than once per battle. [Source: p. 56.]

N5. EXPLOSIVES — 15.05; cost 1 CP.
WHEN: Your Shooting phase.
TARGET: One friendly unengaged EXPLOSIVES/GRENADES unit eligible to shoot that did not advance this turn.
EFFECT:
  1. Select one EXPLOSIVES/GRENADES model in the unit.
  2. Select one unengaged enemy unit visible to and within 8 inches of that model.
  3. Roll six D6; every 4+ inflicts one mortal wound on that enemy unit.
The printed effect does not say this selects the unit to shoot or consumes its shooting activation. [Source: p. 56.]

N6. CRUSHING IMPACT — 15.06; cost 1 CP.
WHEN: Your Charge phase, immediately after a friendly MONSTER/VEHICLE unit ends a charge move.
TARGET: That MONSTER/VEHICLE unit.
EFFECT:
  1. Select one enemy unit engaged with it.
  2. Select one model in your unit engaged with that enemy unit.
  3. Roll D6 equal to that model's T characteristic. Each 1 causes your unit to suffer one mortal wound; each 5+ causes the enemy unit to suffer one mortal wound. Cap the mortal wounds suffered through this effect at six per unit.
[Source: p. 56.]

N7. RAPID INGRESS — 15.07; cost 1 CP.
WHEN: End of the opponent's Movement phase.
TARGET: One friendly unit in strategic reserves, excluding AIRCRAFT.
EFFECT: It makes an ingress move.
RESTRICTION: Cannot be used in the first battle round. Apply the ingress procedure and any relevant abilities. [Source: p. 57.]

N8. FIRE OVERWATCH — 15.08; cost 1 CP.
WHEN: End of the opponent's Movement phase.
TARGET: One friendly unengaged unit, excluding TITANIC units.
EFFECT: It shoots using snap shooting; see I6. [Source: p. 57.]

N9. SMOKESCREEN — 15.10; cost 1 CP.
WHEN: Start of the opponent's Shooting phase.
TARGET: One friendly SMOKE unit.
EFFECT: Until the phase ends, the target of a ranged attack has cover against that attack if it is the selected SMOKE unit, or if it is not fully visible to the attacker because of one or more models in that SMOKE unit. [Source: p. 57.]

N10. HEROIC INTERVENTION — 15.11; base cost 1 CP.
WHEN: End of the opponent's Charge phase.
TARGET: One friendly unengaged unit within 12 inches of at least one enemy unit. A VEHICLE is selectable only if it is a CHARACTER/WALKER unit.
EFFECT: Resolve a charge with the selected unit. Before rolling the charge, choose one mode:
  Leap to Defend: charge targets may only be enemy units that made a charge move this phase and are within the maximum distance. Apply the normal charge procedure too.
  Into the Fray: pay one additional CP. After modifiers, cap the charge-roll result at six. Charge targets may be any enemy units within both 6 inches of your unit and its maximum distance.
Resolve the resulting charge move's conditions and effects as usual. The printed stratagem does not remove the charge move's Fights First effect. [Source: pp. 37, 57.]

N11. COUNTEROFFENSIVE — 15.12; cost 2 CP.
WHEN: The Fight step of the opponent's Fight phase, immediately after an enemy unit resolves its attacks.
TARGET: One friendly unit eligible to fight.
EFFECT: Until the phase ends, it has Fights First and must be the next unit you select to fight. Apply the Fight sequence's priority rules. [Source: p. 57.]

O. ACTIONS

O1. Read an action's instructions — 16.01.
An action specifies when it STARTS, which UNITS can perform it, its USE LIMIT, when it COMPLETES, its completion EFFECT, and additional restrictions. The actual available actions come from other publications such as mission packs. Core actions are available to all players; other actions may have restrictions such as faction. The Deploy Device box in this book is explicitly an illustrative example that cannot be used. [Source: p. 58.]

O2. Check eligibility to start an action — 16.01.
A unit is eligible unless at least one applies:
  1. It is not on the battlefield.
  2. It is AIRCRAFT or FORTIFICATION.
  3. It is battle-shocked.
  4. Its OC is zero or '-'.
  5. It is engaged and is not TITANIC.
  6. It advanced or fell back this turn.
  7. It started another action this turn.
Also apply any action-specific conditions and restrictions from other procedures, such as shooting this phase. [Source: pp. 34-35, 57-58.]

O3. Apply restrictions after starting an action — 16.01.
Until the turn ends, the unit cannot shoot, unless TITANIC, and cannot declare a charge. [Source: p. 58.]

O4. Complete or fail an action — 16.01.
If the performing unit makes a move other than a pile-in or consolidation, or leaves the battlefield, it does not complete the action. Battle-shock also prevents completion. Otherwise, at the action's specified completion time, resolve its EFFECT. Apply its use limit and any additional restrictions. [Source: pp. 9, 58.]

P. MONSTERS, VEHICLES AND FRAME MODELS

P1. Move MONSTER/VEHICLE models through models — 17.01.
During a normal or advance move, MONSTER/VEHICLE models can pass through friendly and enemy models except other MONSTER/VEHICLE models. This permission is tied to those two move types, not every move. [Source: p. 62.]

P2. Measure and rotate FRAME models — 17.02.
Baseless models have FRAME; some other large models have it too. Unless otherwise stated, measure their position relative to other things from the closest point on the model itself, not necessarily the base even if they have one. Rotate a baseless FRAME model upright around its central axis. [Source: p. 62.]

P3. Shoot at engaged MONSTER/VEHICLE units — 17.03.
In your Shooting phase, an engaged enemy MONSTER/VEHICLE unit may be targeted by ranged attacks. Subtract one from the hit roll for an attack against such a target, except a CLOSE-QUARTERS attack made by a model whose unit is engaged with that target. This target permission does not itself make an engaged firing unit eligible to shoot; it still needs an eligible shooting type. BLAST cannot target an engaged MONSTER/VEHICLE under the book's FAQ. [Source: pp. 62-63, 88.]

Q. TRANSPORTS

Q1. Establish transport capacity and starting passengers — 18.01.
Use the transport's datasheet to determine which friendly model types it can carry and its maximum capacity. Multiple units may be embarked at once if capacity permits. In the pre-battle Declare Battle Formations step, units may start inside a friendly transport only if it has sufficient remaining capacity for the entire unit. [Source: p. 64.]

Q2. Embark a unit — 18.02.
After the first battle round has begun, a unit may embark after a normal, advance or fall-back move if:
  1. Every model is within 3 inches of the friendly transport.
  2. The unit was not set up on the battlefield this turn.
  3. Its models satisfy the transport's passenger eligibility.
  4. Enough remaining capacity exists for all its models.
Remove the unit from the battlefield and set it aside as embarked. It is no longer on the battlefield. A scout move does not permit embarking. [Source: pp. 64, 88.]

Q3. Determine when passengers disembark — 18.03.
In the active player's Movement phase, friendly passengers can leave a transport by a disembark move. If a transport is destroyed, before it is removed the active player must make an emergency disembark move with each embarked unit. Apply the temporary active-player role when each passenger unit is selected to move. [Source: pp. 8, 64.]

Q4. Check ordinary disembark eligibility and choose its mode — 18.04.
The unit must be embarked in a transport on the battlefield, must not have embarked in that transport this phase, and the transport must not have advanced or fallen back this phase. Set it up using the general setup procedure, modified below. Choose the first applicable mandatory mode:
  1. Rapid Disembark: if the transport made a normal or ingress move this phase.
  2. Tactical Disembark: otherwise, if the transport remained stationary or has not yet been selected to move this phase, and the tactical setup is possible.
  3. Combat Disembark: otherwise. Make one hazard roll for each model in the passenger unit before setup.
For Rapid or Tactical Disembark, set up each model wholly within 3 inches of the transport. For Combat Disembark, use 6 inches. [Source: p. 65.]

Q5. Apply Rapid Disembark restrictions — 18.04.
If the transport made an ingress move this turn, each passenger must follow the same arrival rules and restrictions the transport followed, including applicable enemy-distance and deployment-zone restrictions. After setup, passengers cannot declare a charge for the remainder of the turn. [Source: p. 65.]

Q6. Apply Tactical Disembark follow-up — 18.04.
After setup, select the passenger unit to make a normal or advance move. Resolve that chosen move's requirements and effects. [Source: p. 65.]

Q7. Apply Combat Disembark exceptions — 18.04.
Each passenger may be set up engaged with enemy units that the transport is engaged with. After setup, the passenger unit is battle-shocked and cannot declare a charge for the remainder of the turn. Other setup conditions still apply. [Source: p. 65.]

Q8. Make an Emergency Disembark move — 18.05.
Eligible unit: embarked in a transport just destroyed. Before setup, make one hazard roll for each model in the passenger unit. Set each model up wholly within 6 inches of that transport and as close as possible to it, under the general setup procedure. Destroy each model that cannot be set up this way. Afterward the passenger unit is battle-shocked and cannot declare a charge for the remainder of the turn. Resolve transport destruction timing and any Deadly Demise in the order stated by those rules. [Source: pp. 65, 80, 87.]

R. ATTACHED UNITS

R1. Form attachments — 19.01; Leader 24.22; Support 24.34.
In the pre-battle Muster Armies step, for each friendly unit with Leader or Support, you may choose one bodyguard unit that it is permitted to lead. The permitted bodyguard units are listed in the app. The chosen leader/support and bodyguard form one attached unit for the battle, treated as one unit for all rules purposes. Unless otherwise stated, a bodyguard can have at most one leader unit and one support unit attached. [Source: p. 66; ability cross-references on pp. 82, 85.]

R2. Resolve attacks against attached units — 19.02.
If any bodyguard models remain, use the highest T among those bodyguard models for attacks targeting the attached unit, even if a leader/support has a different T. If only leader/support models remain, use the highest T among those models. A rule triggered by the unit being destroyed triggers only when the last model that began the battle in that attached unit is destroyed. [Source: p. 66.]

R3. Apply attached-unit keywords — 19.03.
The attached unit has every keyword of its component units and is affected by rules for any of those unit keywords. Individual models retain their own keywords; they do not gain other models' keywords. Attacks target the unit, so an ANTI-PSYKER attack can trigger against the whole unit while it contains a PSYKER leader, even if the eventual allocation is to a non-PSYKER bodyguard. [Source: p. 67.]

R4. Apply attached-unit abilities and their duration — 19.04.
An ability or rule affecting only a specified model remains limited to that model. Other abilities/rules affecting a unit or its models apply throughout the attached unit until their source is destroyed:
  1. Leader/support source: until its last model is destroyed.
  2. Bodyguard source: until its last model is destroyed.
  3. A specific bearer/source model: until that model is destroyed.
A leader/support that started the battle attached keeps benefiting from its own 'while leading a unit' abilities after its bodyguards are destroyed. If a source's last model was destroyed by an attack, keep its conferred ability through the end of all the attacking unit's attacks. If a source is revived, its abilities can apply again. [Source: p. 67.]

S. STRATEGIC RESERVES

S1. Choose pre-battle strategic reserves — 20.01.
During Declare Battle Formations, select friendly units other than FORTIFICATIONS to place in strategic reserves instead of deploying them. Set them aside for later arrival. Unless otherwise stated, their combined points, including passengers inside transports themselves in reserves, must not exceed 50 percent of the battle-size points limit. AIRCRAFT have a mandatory reserves rule. [Source: pp. 68, 74.]

S2. Reposition a unit into reserves — 20.02.
Some rules remove a unit from the battlefield and place it in strategic reserves during the battle. Such a unit is repositioned. In addition to its rule's own arrival conditions:
  1. If the repositioning rule is used in the Movement phase, it can affect a unit that already moved that phase.
  2. If the unit arrives in the same turn as an advance, fall-back or disembark move it previously made, it still counts as having made that move this turn.
  3. Effects with durations or circumstances continue while those durations/circumstances apply. For example, an aura may cease because its range condition no longer holds, while battle-shock remains in the same turn.
[Source: p. 68.]

S3. Arrive from reserves — 20.03.
A strategic reserves unit arrives by making an ingress move. Unless otherwise stated, it can do this only from the second battle round onwards. At the end of the third battle round, destroy every strategic reserves unit that has never made an ingress move, except repositioned units and passengers in transports that have made an ingress move during the battle. [Source: p. 69.]

S4. Make an ingress move — 20.04.
Eligible unit: in strategic reserves, but not a passenger inside a transport itself in reserves. Set the unit up using the general setup procedure. Place it wholly within 6 inches of one or more battlefield edges and more than 8 inches horizontally from every enemy unit. Before the third battle round, no model may be within the opponent's deployment zone. Unless otherwise stated, after arriving the unit cannot make any other type of move until the start of the next Charge phase. Abilities such as Deep Strike can change placement. [Source: pp. 69, 80.]

T. SURGING AND FLYING

T1. Make a surge move — 21.01-21.02.
The granting rule supplies the maximum distance. A unit is eligible only if that rule has triggered, it is not battle-shocked, it is unengaged, and it has not moved this phase.
Before moving, select the closest enemy unit as the surge target. Move under the general movement procedure. Each model must finish engaged with the surge target if possible. If it cannot, it must finish as close as possible to that target. The unit cannot finish engaged with a different enemy unit. After this move it cannot move again this phase. Apply AIRCRAFT exceptions when determining the target. [Source: pp. 70, 74.]

T2. Take to the skies — 21.03.
Models with FLY, and units containing them, are flying models/units. When a flying unit is selected for a normal, advance, fall-back or charge move, before moving any model the active player may declare that it takes to the skies. If so:
  1. Reduce the move's maximum distance by 2 inches, unless Hover overrides this.
  2. For each flying model moved, ignore vertical distance when calculating its movement distance.
  3. That flying model may pass through all models, including enemies and MONSTER/VEHICLE models.
  4. That flying model may move horizontally and vertically through every terrain category.
This changes traversal, not the other eligibility or end-of-move conditions. It is not stated as an automatic permission for every move type. [Source: pp. 71, 82.]

U. OTHER ABILITIES AND AIRCRAFT

U1. Apply aura abilities — 22.01.
An ability affecting models/units within a stated range and tagged Aura is an aura ability. A model on the battlefield is always within its own aura's range. A unit can benefit from different auras simultaneously, but multiple sources of the same aura apply only once. [Source: p. 72.]

U2. Apply faction abilities — 22.02.
Faction abilities, also called army rules, are common faction rules listed on datasheets. Unless otherwise stated, a unit's faction abilities apply only if the faction chosen when mustering the army matches one of that unit's faction keywords. [Source: p. 72.]

U3. Identify psychic abilities — 22.03.
An ability tagged Psychic is a psychic ability. If it causes a model to lose wounds, each such wound is inflicted by a psychic attack for purposes of rules that care about psychic attacks. Apply the separate PSYCHIC weapon ability when attacks use such a weapon. [Source: pp. 72, 84.]

U4. Apply wargear abilities — 22.04.
An ability gained from an item of wargear is a wargear ability. If the unit has that item, the ability applies to that unit. If a specific model has it, that model is its bearer and the ability applies until the bearer is destroyed. Apply attached-unit duration rules where relevant. [Source: pp. 67, 72.]

U5. Apply Plunging Fire — 22.05.
For a ranged attack against a visible unit containing at least one ground-level model, improve BS by one if either the attacker is on a terrain section at least 3 inches high, or the attacker is TOWERING and the target is within 12 inches. Either condition qualifies; satisfying both does not instruct applying it twice. Plunging Fire has no effect on attacks made by or targeting AIRCRAFT. [Source: pp. 73-74.]

U6. Deploy and move AIRCRAFT — 23.01-23.02.
  1. In Declare Battle Formations, place every AIRCRAFT unit in strategic reserves.
  2. AIRCRAFT can make only ingress moves, not other move types.
  3. At the end of the opponent's turn, place all friendly AIRCRAFT currently on the battlefield into strategic reserves.
  4. Models making any move can pass through AIRCRAFT models.
  5. During pile-in, consolidation or surge moves, a unit without FLY ignores AIRCRAFT when selecting enemy units or determining the closest enemy unit.
  6. Being engaged only with AIRCRAFT does not prevent normal or advance move eligibility.
[Source: p. 74.]

U7. Shoot, charge and fight with/against AIRCRAFT — 23.03-23.04.
Plunging Fire does not apply to attacks by or against AIRCRAFT. AIRCRAFT cannot declare charges and may make melee attacks only against flying units. Only flying units may choose AIRCRAFT as charge targets, and only flying models may make melee attacks against AIRCRAFT. [Source: p. 74.]

V. CORE ABILITIES AND WEAPON ABILITIES

V1. Interpret ability names and keyword restrictions — 24.01.
Datasheets list core abilities and weapon profiles list weapon abilities, shown in brackets. A weapon described as a BLAST weapon is one with BLAST. If an ability is followed by keywords, it applies to attacks with that weapon only when the target unit has at least one of those keywords. For example, LETHAL HITS: VEHICLE requires a VEHICLE target; SUSTAINED HITS 1: INFANTRY/BEASTS requires an INFANTRY or BEASTS target. [Source: p. 78.]

V2. Resolve duplicated abilities — 24.02.
Multiple instances of the same core or weapon ability do not accumulate, even if their numbers or keyword restrictions differ. The controller chooses which instance applies at a given time. For weapons, make this choice each time the unit attacks, during Select Weapons. Thus SUSTAINED HITS 1 and 2 are alternatives, not a total of three; ANTI-VEHICLE 4+ and ANTI-INFANTRY 2+ are also duplicated instances requiring a choice.
For Scouts, the book gives a special unit-wide selection rule: select the lowest number not shared by every model. If every model has both Scouts 6 inches and Scouts 8 inches, Scouts 8 inches can be selected because every model shares it. If one model has only Scouts 6 inches and five have only Scouts 8 inches, use Scouts 6 inches. Apply this exception together with the all-model requirement in V31; see PART III, I-02 for combinations not settled by the examples. [Source: p. 78.]

V3. ANTI-X Y+ — 24.03.
If the target unit has keyword X, an attack with this ability critically wounds on an unmodified wound roll of Y or higher. For example ANTI-VEHICLE 4+ critically wounds a VEHICLE unit on unmodified 4+. [Source: p. 79.]

V4. ASSAULT — 24.04.
A unit containing at least one model with an ASSAULT weapon can use assault shooting, following I3. [Source: p. 79.]

V5. BLAST / BLAST X — 24.05.
When gathering dice for a BLAST weapon, add one attack die per complete five models the target had when targets were selected. For BLAST X, add X dice per complete five target models instead. Count at target selection, not after casualties from earlier attacks. For example BLAST 2 against twelve models adds four dice. BLAST cannot target an engaged unit, including an engaged MONSTER/VEHICLE, under the shooting rules and FAQ. [Source: pp. 35, 63, 79, 88.]

V6. CLEAVE X — 24.06.
When gathering dice, if all attacks from that weapon were assigned to a single target, add X attack dice per complete five models in that target at target selection. Splitting the weapon's attacks among multiple units does not meet this condition. [Source: p. 79.]

V7. CLOSE-QUARTERS — 24.07.
A unit containing at least one model with such a weapon can use close-quarters shooting. When using another shooting type, each model other than MONSTER/VEHICLE must choose between using one or more CLOSE-QUARTERS weapons and using one or more of its other ranged weapons; it cannot select both categories for that model. [Source: p. 80.]

V8. Deadly Demise X — 24.08.
When a model with this ability is destroyed, first resolve emergency disembarkation for any passengers. Then roll one D6. On six, every unit within 6 inches of that model suffers X mortal wounds. If X is random, roll it separately for each affected unit. Apply destruction timing: the book's example finishes the attacking unit's unresolved attacks first, then disembarks passengers, then resolves Deadly Demise, then removes the destroyed transport. [Source: pp. 80, 87.]

V9. Deep Strike — 24.09.
When a unit makes an ingress move, if every model in it has Deep Strike, it can be set up anywhere more than 8 inches horizontally from all enemy units, including inside the opponent's deployment zone. This changes placement; the ability does not itself replace the stated reserves arrival timing. [Source: pp. 69, 80.]

V10. DEVASTATING WOUNDS — 24.10.
On a critical wound, end that attack's normal sequence and cause mortal wounds equal to the weapon's D. Inflict them after normal damage from those attacks. These mortal wounds can damage at most one model per critical wound; lose any remainder from that attack after that model is destroyed. For example, a critical wound with Damage 3 against a model with two remaining wounds destroys it and loses the third mortal wound. [Source: p. 80.]

V11. EXTRA ATTACKS — 24.11.
When the unit fights, for each model with these weapons, select all of its EXTRA ATTACKS weapons in addition to one other melee weapon if it has one. [Source: p. 81.]

V12. Feel No Pain X+ — 24.12.
Each time a model would lose a wound, roll one D6. On X or higher, that wound is not lost. Roll per wound that would be lost, not once for the entire damage value. [Source: p. 81.]

V13. Fights First — 24.13.
A unit is a Fights First unit while every model in it has this ability. Resolve its eligibility and selection using the Fights First step in K4. [Source: p. 81.]

V14. Firing Deck X — 24.14.
In your Shooting phase, when this transport is selected to shoot and contains passengers:
  1. Select up to X embarked models, excluding models whose units were already selected to shoot this phase.
  2. Choose one ranged weapon from each selected model, excluding ONE SHOT weapons.
  3. Add these weapons to the transport's weapons until it has resolved all its attacks.
  4. For the remainder of the turn, units embarked in that transport are not eligible to shoot.
[Source: p. 81.]

V15. HAZARDOUS — 24.15.
After a unit selected to shoot or fight finishes all its attacks, make hazard rolls equal to the number of HAZARDOUS weapons selected in its Select Weapons step. Count selected weapons, not attack dice or hits. Use E5 to resolve the rolls. [Source: p. 81.]

V16. HEAVY — 24.16.
In your Shooting phase, add one to an attack's hit roll with a HEAVY weapon if its unit is unengaged, was not set up this turn, and no model in it moved more than 3 inches this turn. All three conditions are required. The book does not require the unit to have remained stationary for this ability. [Source: p. 82.]

V17. Hover — 24.17.
When the unit takes to the skies, do not apply the usual 2-inch reduction in maximum movement distance. [Source: p. 82.]

V18. IGNORES COVER — 24.18.
For an attack with this weapon, the target cannot have the benefit of cover, including cover supplied by non-terrain rules such as Stealth. [Source: p. 82.]

V19. INDIRECT FIRE — 24.19.
A unit with at least one model carrying such a weapon can use indirect shooting. Follow all restrictions in I5. [Source: p. 82.]

V20. Infiltrators — 24.20.
During deployment, a unit whose every model has this ability may be set up anywhere more than 8 inches horizontally from the opponent's deployment zone and every enemy unit. Meet the other setup requirements too. [Source: p. 82.]

V21. LANCE — 24.21.
Add one to the wound roll for an attack with this weapon if the attacking model's unit made a charge move this turn. [Source: p. 82.]

V22. Leader — 24.22.
Use the attached-unit formation and effects in R1-R4. [Source: pp. 66-67, 82.]

V23. LETHAL HITS — 24.23.
On a critical hit, you may choose to have the attack automatically wound. This is optional. If chosen, do not roll to wound; that attack cannot thereby produce a critical wound or trigger critical-wound abilities such as DEVASTATING WOUNDS. [Source: p. 82.]

V24. Lone Operative / Lone Operative X inches — 24.24.
Unless part of an attached unit, the unit is not visible to enemy models unless they are within 12 inches of it, and INDIRECT FIRE cannot target it unless the attacking model is within 12 inches. If the ability supplies X inches, use X instead of twelve for both conditions. Meeting the distance condition does not itself override other visibility/target restrictions. [Source: p. 83.]

V25. MELTA X — 24.25.
If the target was within half the weapon's range when targets were selected, add X to its D until the attacking unit finishes its attacks. For example D6 damage with MELTA 2 becomes D6+2. [Source: p. 83.]

V26. ONE SHOT — 24.26.
Each such weapon may be selected to make attacks only once per battle. Reviving its destroyed bearer does not reset that weapon's use. If an entirely new unit is added to the army, its ONE SHOT weapons may each be used once. [Source: p. 83.]

V27. PISTOL — 24.27.
PISTOL and CLOSE-QUARTERS are identical for all rules purposes. Use V7 for either label. [Source: p. 83.]

V28. PRECISION — 24.28.
When resolving attacks with one or more PRECISION weapons, at the start of Allocation Order, if the target includes CHARACTER models visible to at least one attacking model, the active player may select an allocation group containing one such visible CHARACTER. If selected, that group becomes current until these attacks are resolved or that group is destroyed, whichever comes first. This changes the current group rather than instructing ordinary bodyguard-first allocation for those attacks. [Source: p. 84.]

V29. PSYCHIC — 24.29.
For an attack with this weapon, you may ignore any or all modifiers to its BS/WS and any or all modifiers to its hit roll. Its attacks are psychic attacks for other rules that refer to them. [Source: p. 84.]

V30. RAPID FIRE X — 24.30.
When gathering dice, add X attack dice for the weapon if the target was within half range at target selection. [Source: p. 84.]

V31. Scouts X inches — 24.31.
During Resolve Pre-battle Abilities, if every model in the unit has Scouts, you may do one of the following:
  1. If it is in strategic reserves, set it up wholly within your deployment zone.
  2. If it is wholly within your deployment zone, make a scout move with it.
  3. If it is embarked in a DEDICATED TRANSPORT wholly within your deployment zone and every model embarked in that transport has Scouts, make a scout move with that transport.
Apply duplicated-Scouts selection as in V2. The pre-battle timing does not permit embarking after this move. [Source: pp. 78, 84, 88.]

V32. Scout move — 24.32.
Eligible unit: in Resolve Pre-battle Abilities and wholly within your deployment zone, with permission to make this move. Maximum distance: the X inches from Scouts. Move under section C and finish more than 8 inches horizontally from every enemy unit. [Source: p. 84.]

V33. Stealth — 24.33.
If every model in the unit has Stealth, it has cover against each ranged attack targeting it. Apply the cover BS change, subject to IGNORES COVER. [Source: pp. 50, 82, 85.]

V34. Support — 24.34.
Use the attached-unit formation and effects in R1-R4. [Source: pp. 66-67, 85.]

V35. Super-heavy Walker — 24.35.
During a normal, advance or fall-back move:
  1. Models in the unit may move through models, including MONSTER/VEHICLE models, but not TITANIC models.
  2. They may move horizontally through terrain sections no more than 4 inches high.
  3. Before moving, you may give every model MOBILE until the move ends. If you do, roll one D6 when it ends; on one, the unit becomes battle-shocked. MOBILE enables horizontal passage through dense terrain under L3.
[Source: p. 85.]

V36. SUSTAINED HITS X — 24.36.
A critical hit with this weapon produces X additional hits. The original critical hit still counts as a hit. SUSTAINED HITS 2 therefore produces three hits in total from that critical hit. [Source: p. 85.]

V37. TORRENT — 24.37.
An attack with this weapon automatically hits. The ability does not instruct a hit roll. [Source: p. 85.]

V38. TWIN-LINKED — 24.38.
You may re-roll the wound roll for an attack with this weapon. [Source: p. 85.]

W. APPENDIX DEFINITIONS AND FAQS

W1. Determine starting strength and half-strength.
A unit's starting strength is its number of models at the start of the first battle round. For an attached unit, count all its models together at that time. Preserve that attached-unit starting strength even if only its leader remains.
If starting strength is one:
  Below starting strength means remaining wounds are less than W.
  At half-strength means remaining wounds equal half W.
  Below half-strength means remaining wounds are less than half W.
If starting strength is at least two:
  Below starting strength means remaining model count is less than starting strength.
  At half-strength means remaining model count equals half starting strength.
  Below half-strength means remaining model count is less than half starting strength.
An odd W or odd starting model count cannot be exactly at half-strength, but can be below half-strength. An attached six-model unit consisting of five bodyguards and a Captain is at half-strength at three models, below at two, and its lone surviving Captain remains below half-strength even at full wounds. [Source: p. 86.]

W2. Add a new unit during the battle.
For a rule adding a new unit, determine its starting strength when it is added, using the same principles as for other units. [Source: p. 87.]

W3. Resolve destruction and removal.
A unit is destroyed when all its models are destroyed. When a model is destroyed, resolve rules triggered by its destruction, then remove it. If such rules apply and the model was destroyed by an attack, unless otherwise stated defer those rules and its removal until the attacking unit has resolved its attacks. Unless otherwise stated, destroyed models/units cannot use abilities or be selected or targeted by rules. Apply the special attached-unit destruction trigger and the coherency-removal exception separately. [Source: pp. 14, 66, 87.]

W4. Move models with different M characteristics.
For a normal or advance move, each model uses its own M to calculate its maximum distance. For example a unit can contain models with a 6-inch normal limit and another with a 9-inch limit. All other unit-move conditions, including coherency, still apply. [Source: p. 87.]

W5. Pass when eligible fighters cannot reach enemies.
When the Fight sequence returns to a player for a selection, if all that player's eligible units are more than 5 inches from every enemy unit, the player may pass and let the opponent select instead. If both players pass consecutively, or a player passes when the opponent has no eligible units left, the Fight step ends. This allows a unit whose targets were destroyed to wait in case later enemy movement puts a new target within reach. [Source: p. 87.]

W6. Use an objective marker outside terrain.
If an objective point does not coincide with a terrain area, centre a flat circular marker 40 millimetres in diameter on that point. Models can move through and finish on top of it. A model is within range if within 3 inches horizontally and 5 inches vertically of the marker. Measure to/from the marker's closest part. Use the same objective-control procedure for this objective. [Source: p. 88.]

W7. Apply mixed keywords.
A unit has all the keywords of all its models, including mixed keywords due to attachments, datasheet distinctions or wargear. Individual models do not gain keywords from the other models. Remember that attacks target units, not individual models. [Source: pp. 67, 87.]

W8. Revive models.
When instructed to revive, resurrect or return models, add the specified destroyed models back to their unit, without exceeding its starting strength. They retain the wargear and enhancements they began with and return at full wounds unless otherwise stated.
For a unit on the battlefield:
  1. The source requires coherency with models that started the phase on the battlefield and parenthetically describes them as already present when the rule was used. Preserve both statements; see PART III, I-01 for the timing ambiguity.
  2. Returned models may be engaged only with enemies already engaged with their unit.
A revived leader/support that belonged to an attached unit still belongs to it and must return to it if possible. ONE SHOT use is not reset by revival. [Source: pp. 83, 88.]

W9. Apply the supplied FAQs.
  No ranged weapons: the unit is still eligible to shoot in its controller's Shooting phase, although it cannot make ranged attacks. This distinction can matter to other rules, including actions.
  BLAST in close-quarters shooting: MONSTER/VEHICLE models cannot use BLAST against an engaged unit.
  BLAST against engaged MONSTER/VEHICLE targets: models cannot target those units with BLAST.
  Overrun eligibility: a unit must also be eligible to fight before being selected for an overrun. A unit that became engaged only after the Fight step began, then became unengaged again, can lack fight eligibility if no other condition qualifies it.
  Embarking after Scouts: not allowed. The Scout move happens during Resolve Pre-battle Abilities, after Declare Battle Formations but before Begin the Battle; it is not an eligible embark move after the first round begins.
[Source: p. 88.]

[P08-01; Source: p. 8]
Before you learn how to move your warriors across the battlefield and attack the enemy in deadly firefights and bloody close combat, this section introduces some core concepts that underpin every Warhammer 40,000 battle.

[P08-02; Source: p. 8]
ARMIES 01.01

[P08-03; Source: p. 8]
Each player in a game of Warhammer 40,000 commands an army made up of units of models. You control all of the models in your army. If a rule refers to the ‘controlling player’, it is referring to the player who controls the models being affected by that rule.

[P08-04; Source: p. 8]
UNITS AND MODELS 01.02

[P08-05; Source: p. 8]
A unit can contain one or more models. These models move and fight together as a single group. Most models have a base, which is also part of that model for all rules purposes.

[P08-06; Source: p. 8]
Rules sometimes affect ‘friendly’ or ‘enemy’ models or units, which are defined as follows:

[P08-07; Source: p. 8]
▪ Friendly units and models are those in your army.

[P08-08; Source: p. 8]
▪ Enemy units and models are those in your opponent’s army.

[P08-09; Source: p. 8]
If a rule affects units or models without specifying that they are friendly or enemy, that rule affects any unit or model, regardless of whose army they are in. When an effect or ability applies to a unit, it applies to every model in that unit.

[P08-10; Source: p. 8]
ACTIVE PLAYER AND OPPOSING PLAYER 01.03

[P08-11; Source: p. 8]
At any given time, one player is the ‘active player’ and their opponent is the ‘opposing player’. Which player is which changes throughout the battle, but both players are always one or the other; whenever a player becomes the active player, their opponent becomes the opposing player, and vice versa.

[P08-12; Source: p. 8]
While it is neither player’s turn (e.g. at the start or end of the battle round), the player who takes the first turn in each battle round is the active player.

[P08-13; Source: p. 8]
While it is a player’s turn, that player is the active player, with the following exceptions:

[P08-14; Source: p. 8]
▪ Each time a unit is selected to move, that unit’s controlling player is the active player until that move ends.
▪ Each time a unit is selected to shoot or selected to fight, that unit’s controlling player is the active player until those attacks are resolved.

MEASURING DISTANCES 01.04

[P09-02; Source: p. 9]
Distances in Warhammer 40,000 are measured in inches ("). You can measure distances whenever you want to.

[P09-03; Source: p. 9]
When a rule refers to a model’s position in relation to anything else on the battlefield, unless otherwise stated, measure to or from the closest part of that model’s base.

[P09-04; Source: p. 9]
DICE 01.05

[P09-05; Source: p. 9]
You will require some six‑sided dice (often abbreviated to D6). There are many ways dice rolls are referred to, including:

[P09-06; Source: p. 9]
▪ 2+, 3+, and so on: 2+ means a result of 2 or more, 3+ means a result of 3 or more, and so on.
▪ 1‑3, 4‑6, and so on: Any result within the specified range will interact with the rule stated.
▪ 2D6, 3D6, and so on: Roll the stated number of D6 and add the individual values together (e.g. to roll 2D6, roll two D6 and add the values together).
▪ D3: Roll one D6 and halve the result (rounding up).

[P09-07; Source: p. 9]
▪ D6+1, 2D6+3, and so on: Roll the stated number of dice and add the stated value to the result.

[P09-08; Source: p. 9]
LEADERSHIP ROLLS 01.06

[P09-09; Source: p. 9]
To make a leadership roll for a unit, its controlling player rolls 2D6: if the result is equal to or greater than one or more of the Ld characteristics in that unit, that roll succeeds. Otherwise, that roll fails. The rule that instructed you to make that leadership roll will describe the effects of that roll succeeding or failing.

[P09-10; Source: p. 9]
BATTLE-SHOCK ROLLS 01.07

[P09-11; Source: p. 9]
To make a battle-shock roll for a unit, its controlling player makes a leadership roll for it (see above).

[P09-12; Source: p. 9]
▪ If that roll succeeds, that unit does not become battle-shocked.
▪ If that roll fails, that unit, and each model in it, is battle-shocked.

[P09-13; Source: p. 9]
While a unit is battle-shocked:

[P09-14; Source: p. 9]
▪ The Objective Control (OC) characteristic of all of its models is modified to ‘-’ (02.02).
▪ Its controlling player cannot target that unit with stratagems (15).
▪ It is not eligible to start an action (16), and any action it has started cannot be completed.

[P12-01; Source: p. 12]
During a battle, you will move your models by picking them up and changing their position on the battlefield. The principles of movement are explained here.

[P12-02; Source: p. 12]
MOVING UNITS 03.01

[P12-03; Source: p. 12]
There are several types of move a unit can make. Each one defines which units are eligible to make it, what its maximum distance or set-up distance is, and any conditions that must be met.

[P12-04; Source: p. 12]
Each time you move a unit, you can move one or more of its models, one at a time, by moving it in a straight line and/or rotating it, as many times as you want to.

[P12-05; Source: p. 12]
Each time you move a model, unless otherwise stated:

[P12-06; Source: p. 12]
▪ It can be moved through friendly models.

[P12-07; Source: p. 12]
▪ It can be moved through any space its base can fit through.

[P12-08; Source: p. 12]
▪ Its base cannot be moved through enemy models.

[P12-09; Source: p. 12]
▪ Its base cannot cross the edge of the battlefield.

[P12-10; Source: p. 12]
▪ All stated ‘While Moving’ conditions must be met.

[P12-11; Source: p. 12]
MOVING A MODEL IN A STRAIGHT LINE

[P12-12; Source: p. 12]
Each time you move a model in a straight line, move it horizontally across the battlefield. Measure from the same point on its base at the start and end of that move, and add that distance to any other distance it has moved since its unit began that move. The distance moved cannot be greater than the maximum distance of that move type.

[P12-13; Source: p. 12]
ROTATING A MODEL

[P12-14; Source: p. 12]
Each time you rotate a model, turn it any amount around the centre of its base, while keeping it upright. Note that rotating a model does not count towards the distance it has moved.

[P12-15; Source: p. 12]
ENDING A MOVE

[P12-16; Source: p. 12]
After you have finished setting up all of the models in a unit and/or moving all of the models in a unit that you want to move, check that all of the following apply:

[P12-17; Source: p. 12]
▪ If that unit is on the battlefield, it is in coherency (03.03).

[P12-18; Source: p. 12]
▪ No models in that unit are on another model or partway through a surface of a terrain feature (e.g. a wall or ceiling).
▪ All stated ‘After Moving’ conditions are met.

[P12-19; Source: p. 12]
If one or more of the above conditions are not met, that unit cannot make that move and its models are returned to their positions at the start of that move. Otherwise, after resolving any additional rules stated in the ‘After Moving’ section of that move type, that move ends. 03

[P14-01; Source: p. 14]
SET UP 03.02

[P14-02; Source: p. 14]
Before your units can move and make attacks, they will first need to be set up on the battlefield. This most commonly happens when deploying your army for battle, but can happen for other reasons as well.

[P14-03; Source: p. 14]
Whenever a rule instructs you to set up a unit, place its models on the battlefield so that:

[P14-04; Source: p. 14]
▪ That unit is in coherency (see below).

[P14-05; Source: p. 14]
▪ That unit is unengaged (see below).

[P14-06; Source: p. 14]
▪ All other requirements and restrictions are met.

[P14-07; Source: p. 14]
If you cannot set up all of the models in a unit, remove that unit from the battlefield and return it to its original position (e.g. in strategic reserves or embarked within a TRANSPORT).

[P14-08; Source: p. 14]
COHERENCY 03.03

[P14-09; Source: p. 14]
A unit that contains more than one model must be set up and end any kind of move in coherency. A unit is in coherency while both of the following apply to every model in that unit:

[P14-10; Source: p. 14]
▪ Within 2" horizontally and 5" vertically of at least one other model in that unit.
▪ Within 9" horizontally and 5" vertically of every other model in that unit.

[P14-11; Source: p. 14]
REGAINING COHERENCY

[P14-12; Source: p. 14]
In the End of Turn step of each player’s turn, if one or more units on the battlefield are not in coherency, those units’ controlling players must remove models from them, one at a time, until they are in coherency again. Models removed in this way are destroyed, but they do not trigger rules that apply when a model is destroyed.

[P14-13; Source: p. 14]
ENGAGEMENT 03.04

[P14-14; Source: p. 14]
A model’s engagement range is the area of the battlefield within 2" horizontally and 5" vertically of it.

[P14-15; Source: p. 14]
▪ While a friendly model is within engagement range of one or more enemy models, those models – and the units they belong to – are engaged with each other.
▪ While a unit contains no engaged models, that unit is unengaged.

[P14-16; Source: p. 14]
WHAT IS COHERENCY? Coherency prevents models in the same unit from becoming too separated from each other while on the battlefield, ensuring they are set up and end every kind of move as a single group.

[P14-17; Source: p. 14]
WHAT IS ENGAGEMENT? While opposing models are within each other’s engagement range, they are able to fight in vicious melee, so unless they are seeking to make melee attacks, models should keep out of their foes’ reach.

[P14-18; Source: p. 14]
IF YOU CANNOT SET UP A UNIT This section explains how to set up your units on the battlefield. While doing so as the result of a move type, if you have to return a unit’s models to their previous positions because it is not possible to set them all up, that unit has not been selected to make that move. This means it can be selected to make a move later, so could either attempt another set up or remain stationary (09.04).

3. RESOLVE ATTACKS 04.03

[P17-02; Source: p. 17]
Resolve attacks using the following sequence:

[P17-03; Source: p. 17]
1. Select Enemy Unit: Select one of the enemy units targeted by one or more weapons.
2. Gather Attack Dice: Select one weapon targeting that unit that has not yet been used to make attacks against it, and gather a number of D6 equal to that weapon’s A characteristic. These are attack dice – each one represents one attack being made by an attacking model with that weapon. If one or more other weapons targeting that unit make identical attacks to the selected weapon (see below) and those weapons have not yet been used to make attacks against that target, they do so now and you gather those weapons’ attack dice as well (e.g. for three weapons making identical attacks, each with an A characteristic of 2, gather a total of six D6).
3. Resolve Attack Dice: Resolve the attack sequence (05) for all of the attack dice you just gathered.
4. Other Attacks: Follow the first of the instructions below that applies:

[P17-04; Source: p. 17]
-> If there are any weapons targeting the same unit that have not yet been used to make attacks, return to the Gather Attack Dice step.

[P17-05; Source: p. 17]
-> Otherwise, if there are any weapons with unresolved attacks targeting a different unit, return to the Select Enemy Unit step.

[P17-06; Source: p. 17]
-> Otherwise, if all weapons have been used to make all of their attacks, this sequence ends and the attacking unit’s attacks have been resolved.

3. SAVE ROLLS 05.03

[P19-02; Source: p. 19]
The opposing player resolves the following sequence:

[P19-03; Source: p. 19]
1. Create Groups: Divide all models in the target unit into the following groups, as many times as required:

[P19-04; Source: p. 19]
▫ One group for each CHARACTER model.

[P19-05; Source: p. 19]
▫ One group for all other models with the same W, Sv and InSv characteristics.
2. Allocation Order: Declare the order in which those groups will have attacks allocated to them, applying all of the following:

[P19-06; Source: p. 19]
▫ If a non-CHARACTER group contains a model that has lost one or more wounds, that group must be first in the allocation order.
▫ No CHARACTER group can be earlier in the allocation order than a non-CHARACTER group.
▫ CHARACTER groups containing a model that has lost one or more wounds must be earlier in the allocation order than CHARACTER groups containing no wounded models.
3. Make Save Rolls: The opposing player makes one save roll for each attack that wounded the target by rolling one D6.

[P19-07; Source: p. 19]
CURRENT ALLOCATION GROUP The first group in the allocation order begins as the current group. Once all models in an allocation group are destroyed, the next group in the allocation order becomes the current one.

[P19-08; Source: p. 19]
4. INFLICT DAMAGE 05.04

[P19-09; Source: p. 19]
The opposing player resolves the following sequence for each save roll, working from lowest result(s) to highest result(s), until all attacks are resolved or all models in the target unit are destroyed – in the latter case, any excess attacks are lost.

[P19-10; Source: p. 19]
1. Select Model: Select one model in the current allocation group (see right); this must be a model that has lost one or more wounds if possible.
2. Check Save Roll: For each result, check if that attack inflicts damage or fails by matching the first condition below that applies:

[P19-11; Source: p. 19]
Save-result conditions, in the printed order (dice icon expanded to a number):
Unmodified 1 -> INFLICTS DAMAGE
Invulnerable Save: The models in the current allocation group have an InSv characteristic, and the result is equal to or greater than that characteristic. -> FAILS
Save and AP: After modifying the result by the attacking weapon’s AP characteristic, it is equal to or greater than the Sv characteristic of models in the current allocation group. -> FAILS
Any other result -> INFLICTS DAMAGE

[P19-12; Source: p. 19]
Example: An AP characteristic of -1 would modify a save roll of 3 to a 2. For models with a Sv characteristic of 2+ or better, that attack would fail.

[P19-13; Source: p. 19]
3. Resolve Damage: If that attack inflicts damage, the selected model loses a number of wounds equal to that attack’s D characteristic. If this reduces that model’s remaining wounds to 0 or fewer, it is destroyed.

[P24-01; Source: p. 24]
This section contains some additional rules concepts that are most frequently used while making attacks.

[P24-02; Source: p. 24]
VISIBILITY 06.01

[P24-03; Source: p. 24]
Line of sight is used to determine visibility between models. For an observing model to have line of sight, it must be possible to draw an imaginary straight line, 1 mm wide, from any part of that model to any part of the model being observed. This line is the line of sight. While doing so, other models in the observing model’s unit and in the observed model’s unit are ignored.

[P24-04; Source: p. 24]
Other models and units can be either visible or fully visible to the observing model, as shown opposite. Note that terrain applies additional rules to visibility (13.07).

[P24-05; Source: p. 24]
MORTAL WOUNDS 06.02

[P24-06; Source: p. 24]
Some attacks or rules inflict mortal wounds on units. Each time a unit suffers one or more mortal wounds, its controlling player must resolve the following sequence for each of those mortal wounds, until either all of them have been inflicted or that unit is destroyed:

[P24-07; Source: p. 24]
1. Select Model: Select one model in that unit by following the first instruction below that applies:

[P24-08; Source: p. 24]
-> If a non‑CHARACTER model in that unit has lost one or more wounds, you must select that model.

[P24-09; Source: p. 24]
-> Otherwise, if that unit contains one or more non‑CHARACTER models, you must select one of those models.

[P24-10; Source: p. 24]
-> Otherwise, if one or more CHARACTER models in that unit have lost one or more wounds, you must select one of those models.

[P24-11; Source: p. 24]
-> Otherwise, you must select one CHARACTER model in that unit.

[P24-12; Source: p. 24]
2. Resolve Damage: The selected model loses 1 wound. If this reduces that model’s remaining wounds to 0, it is destroyed.

[P24-13; Source: p. 24]
MORTAL WOUNDS AND NORMAL DAMAGE

[P24-14; Source: p. 24]
When resolving attack dice, if those attacks inflict a mixture of both mortal wounds and normal damage, resolve all of the normal damage first, then resolve all of the mortal wounds.

[P24-15; Source: p. 24]
HAZARD ROLLS 06.03

[P24-16; Source: p. 24]
To make a hazard roll for a unit, roll one D6: on a 1-2, that roll fails and that unit suffers 1 mortal wound (see above), or 3 mortal wounds instead if each model in that unit is a MONSTER/VEHICLE model. If more than one hazard roll is required for a unit, make all of those rolls simultaneously.

[P28-02; Source: p. 28]
At the start of the battle round, players resolve rules that are triggered at the start of the battle round, before progressing to player turns.

[P28-04; Source: p. 28]

2. PLAYER TURNS
3. END OF BATTLE ROUND

[P28-05; Source: p. 28]
Games of Warhammer 40,000 are played in a series of battle rounds. This section describes the structure of the battle round, defining the order in which players take turns to move their units and make attacks with them.

[P28-06; Source: p. 28]
2. PLAYER TURNS 07.02

[P28-07; Source: p. 28]
Both players now take one turn each. The same player always takes the first turn in each battle round – the mission you are playing will tell you which player this is. Once that player’s turn has ended, their opponent takes their turn.

[P28-08; Source: p. 28]
Each turn consists of seven parts: first the Start of Turn step, then a series of five phases resolved in the order shown opposite, then the End of Turn step.

[P28-09; Source: p. 28]
3. END OF BATTLE ROUND 07.03

[P28-10; Source: p. 28]
Rules that are triggered at the end of the battle round are resolved now, in the following order:

[P28-11; Source: p. 28]
1. First resolve rules triggered at this point other than mission rules.
2. Both players then consult their mission; if one or both players have achieved any aspects of their mission that are triggered at this point, resolve them now.

[P28-12; Source: p. 28]
The battle round then ends and, unless the battle ends, the next battle round starts. The mission you are playing will tell you how many battle rounds to resolve before the battle ends.

[P46-01; Source: p. 46]
PLACING TERRAIN 13.01

[P46-02; Source: p. 46]
Before the battle, place a selection of terrain features on the battlefield using one or more of the following methods:

[P46-03; Source: p. 46]
▪ Place a well‑defined boundary (such as a base or mat) in each location you want to furnish with terrain, then place one or more terrain features wholly within that boundary.
▪ Place one terrain feature directly on the battlefield.

[P46-04; Source: p. 46]
▪ Place two or more terrain features directly on the battlefield in such a way that together they define the boundary of an area.

[P46-05; Source: p. 46]
In each case, the area of the battlefield occupied by that boundary or terrain feature is known as a terrain area. A mission’s deployment map may define the location and dimensions of each terrain area. Otherwise, players must agree on this before the battle.

[P46-06; Source: p. 46]
TERRAIN CATEGORIES 13.02

[P46-07; Source: p. 46]
Each terrain feature belongs to a terrain category, which can affect the movement and visibility of models. Below are some examples of which types of terrain features fall under each category.

[P46-08; Source: p. 46]
EXPOSED 13.03

[P46-09; Source: p. 46]
Exposed terrain offers only scant protection to the most desperate troops, and can be traversed without hindrance. Examples: Craters, razorwire, scattered debris.

[P46-10; Source: p. 46]
LIGHT 13.04

[P46-11; Source: p. 46]
Light terrain can provide cover from incoming attacks, but will not slow an enemy’s advance or offer lasting defence. Examples: Barricades, low walls, statuary.

[P46-12; Source: p. 46]
DENSE 13.05

[P46-13; Source: p. 46]
Dense terrain is an obstacle to even the largest war machines, and can shelter entire squads from enemy sight. Examples: Buildings, ruins, armoured containers, woods.

[P46-14; Source: p. 46]
A mission’s deployment map may define which terrain categories should be present within each terrain area. Meeting these requirements will create the best gaming experience. Note that terrain features sharing the same terrain area can belong to different terrain categories.

[P46-15; Source: p. 46]
This section provides rules for using terrain to transform your gaming table into an interactive, thematic battlefield. These rules help to bring your battlefield to life and introduce a vital tactical dimension to your games.

[P46-16; Source: p. 46]
CREATING YOUR OWN BATTLEFIELD Don’t worry if your battlefield doesn’t match a mission’s terrain category requirements; just do the best with the terrain features you have. If a mission does not provide you with guidance, it will be up to the players to decide how to create their battlefield. When doing so, keep in mind that dense terrain features have the greatest impact on visibility and movement. Battlefields with too few dense terrain features can advantage armies that rely on shooting, or disadvantage armies that rely on melee. Also remember to leave sufficient room around dense terrain features for larger models such as MONSTERS/VEHICLES to manoeuvre, especially near the edges of the battlefield.

TERRAIN AND MOVEMENT 13.06

[P48-02; Source: p. 48]
Models can move through different categories of terrain feature as follows:

[P48-03; Source: p. 48]
▪ Exposed/Light: All models can move horizontally and vertically through exposed and light terrain features.
▪ Dense:

[P48-04; Source: p. 48]
▫ INFANTRY/BEASTS/SWARM/MOBILE models can move horizontally through dense terrain features.
▫ INFANTRY/BEASTS/SWARM models can move vertically through dense terrain features.
▫ Other models can move horizontally through dense terrain features provided that all sections of that terrain feature that the moving model’s base would move through are 2" or less in height. Otherwise, the moving model must move vertically to ascend or descend such sections. They cannot move through ceilings and floors while doing so, and they cannot end that move on any surface of that terrain feature that is not on ground level (see below).

[P48-05; Source: p. 48]
MOVING VERTICALLY

[P48-06; Source: p. 48]
Models can move vertically to ascend or descend terrain features. While doing so:

[P48-07; Source: p. 48]
▪ That model must remain within ½" horizontally of that terrain feature.
▪ Add the distance moved vertically up, and the distance moved vertically down, to any other distance that model has moved since its unit began that move.

[P48-08; Source: p. 48]
SETTING UP OR ENDING A MOVE

[P48-09; Source: p. 48]
Models can be set up or end a move on the ground level of terrain features. Models can also be set up or end a move on any surface of a terrain feature that is not on ground level, if all of the following apply:

[P48-10; Source: p. 48]
▪ That model has one or more of the following keywords: INFANTRY/BEASTS/SWARM/FLY/MONSTER.
▪ After ending that move, that model is stable and no part of its base overhangs the outer edge of that surface.

[P48-11; Source: p. 48]
PLUNGING FIRE As well as gaining superior lines of sight, models that ascend tall terrain features can benefit from the Plunging Fire rule (22.05).

[P48-12; Source: p. 48]
SOLID TERRAIN If a terrain feature has the Solid rule (13.11), as dense terrain features do, a model cannot end a move such that any part of it is through any enclosed part of that terrain feature that is 3" or less from ground level – not even through small openings such as doors and windows. This ensures that protruding elements of models cannot be used to circumvent the visibility restrictions of the Solid rule.

TERRAIN AND VISIBILITY 13.07

[P50-02; Source: p. 50]
Terrain can affect visibility, depending on whether the Benefit of Cover, Hidden, Obscuring or Solid rules apply.

[P50-03; Source: p. 50]
BENEFIT OF COVER 13.08

[P50-04; Source: p. 50]
Each time a ranged attack targets a unit, if every model in that unit meets one or more of the following conditions, that unit has the benefit of cover against that attack:

[P50-05; Source: p. 50]
▪ That model has the INFANTRY/BEASTS/SWARM keyword and is within a terrain area.
▪ That model is not fully visible to the attacking model due to one or more intervening terrain features and/or one or more intervening obscuring terrain areas (see below).

[P50-06; Source: p. 50]
Each time a ranged attack targets a unit that has the benefit of cover against it, worsen the BS characteristic of that attack by 1.

[P50-07; Source: p. 50]
HIDDEN 13.09

[P50-08; Source: p. 50]
A model is hidden while all of the following apply to it:

[P50-09; Source: p. 50]
▪ That model has the INFANTRY/BEASTS/SWARM keyword and is within a terrain area that contains one or more dense terrain features.
▪ That model’s unit did not make one or more ranged attacks during this turn or during the previous turn.

[P50-10; Source: p. 50]
While a model is hidden, it can only be visible to enemy models that are within its detection range. Unless otherwise stated, a model’s detection range is 15".

[P50-11; Source: p. 50]
OBSCURING 13.10

[P50-12; Source: p. 50]
Terrain areas containing one or more light or dense terrain features are obscuring terrain areas. If every line of sight drawn between two models crosses one or more obscuring terrain areas (excluding obscuring terrain areas that one or both of those models are within), those two models are not visible to each other.

[P50-13; Source: p. 50]
SOLID 13.11

[P50-14; Source: p. 50]
Dense terrain features have the Solid rule. Line of sight cannot be drawn across any enclosed gap in the surface of such a terrain feature that is 3" or less from ground level.

[P50-15; Source: p. 50]
Designer’s Note: This rule ensures that models are not visible while sheltering in ground-level terrain, irrespective of small openings such as doors, windows or bullet holes, or because of small gaps between adjacent terrain features. 3" is the height of the first floor of many terrain features, but some missions may adjust the height at which this rule takes effect.

HIDDEN AND OBSCURING BENEFIT OF COVER

[P51-02; Source: p. 51]
▪ There is an obscuring terrain area between all models in units A and D, so none of them are visible to each other.

[P51-03; Source: p. 51]

However, unit D’s models are within the 15" detection range of unit B’s models (but not unit C’s), so unit B’s models are visible to unit D’s. Unit D’s models are also visible to the models in units B and C, since they are not within a terrain area.

[P51-04; Source: p. 51]
Units A and B are not visible to each other, because even though there is a window in this dense terrain feature, it has the Solid rule, so line of sight cannot be drawn through such enclosed gaps that are 3" or less from ground level.

[P51-05; Source: p. 51]
Units A and C are visible to each other, because even though this dense terrain feature has the Solid rule, line of sight can be drawn normally through enclosed gaps that are more than 3" from ground level.

TERRAIN OBJECTIVES 14.01

[P52-02; Source: p. 52]
If a mission uses objectives, it will state where they are located on the battlefield. Typically, your mission will have a deployment map showing several points where objectives should be placed. The location of each point should coincide with a terrain area (13.01); that terrain area is the objective, and is called a terrain objective.

[P52-03; Source: p. 52]
When measuring distances to and from an objective, measure to and from the closest part of it.

[P52-04; Source: p. 52]
LEVEL OF CONTROL 14.02

[P52-05; Source: p. 52]
At the start of the battle, no objective on the battlefield is controlled by either player. To gain control of an objective, a player will need one or more models with an OC characteristic of 1 or more within range of it. A model is within range of a terrain objective while it is within that terrain area.

[P52-06; Source: p. 52]
At the end of each phase and turn, to determine a player’s level of control over an objective, add together the OC characteristics of all the models in that player’s army that are within range of that objective:

[P52-07; Source: p. 52]
▪ The player who has the highest level of control over that objective controls that objective.
▪ If both players have the same level of control over that objective, unless that objective is secured (see below), that objective is not controlled by either player.

[P52-08; Source: p. 52]
While one or more units from a player’s army are within range of an objective that player controls, for each of those units that contains one or more models with an OC characteristic of 1 or more, that unit is said to be controlling that objective.

[P52-09; Source: p. 52]
SECURED OBJECTIVES 14.03

[P52-10; Source: p. 52]
Some rules allow an objective to be secured by a player’s army. When an objective is secured by a player’s army, that objective remains under their control – even once they no longer have any units within range of it – until their opponent’s level of control over that objective is greater than theirs at the end of a phase.

[P52-11; Source: p. 52]
HOLD AT ALL COSTS Some units have abilities that are triggered when they control an objective, or when they secure an objective for your army.

[P52-12; Source: p. 52]
Objectives represent critical locations that both sides are attempting to secure, and many missions require your forces to take control of them to achieve victory. This section describes how to do so with your models.

[P54-02; Source: p. 54]
During the battle, both players can use stratagems. Each stratagem states:

[P54-03; Source: p. 54]

[P54-04; Source: p. 54]

[P54-05; Source: p. 54]

[P54-06; Source: p. 54]

[P54-07; Source: p. 54]

[P54-08; Source: p. 54]

[P54-09; Source: p. 54]
▪ Each player cannot use the same stratagem more than once in the same phase.

[P54-10; Source: p. 54]

[P54-11; Source: p. 54]
1. Select targets as described in that stratagem.
2. Reduce your CP total by the CP cost listed in that stratagem. If that stratagem contains a section that carries an additional CP cost (e.g. +1CP), you can only use that additional section if you increase the CP cost of that stratagem by the amount shown. If you do not have enough CP remaining, you cannot use that stratagem.
3. Resolve the effects of that stratagem.

They are used when a player deems that a critical point in the battle is reached, where extra combat prowess or fleeting but powerful effects are required to tip the balance in their favour.

[P66-01; Source: p. 66]
FORMING ATTACHED UNITS 19.01

[P66-02; Source: p. 66]
Some units have the Leader or Support ability listed on their datasheet (see below). Such units are known as leader units and support units respectively. Both of these abilities allow such units to lead other friendly units (known as bodyguard units) to form attached units. An attached unit is a single unit for all rules purposes. Leader and support units can only lead specific bodyguard units, as listed in the Warhammer 40,000 app.

[P66-03; Source: p. 66]
LEADER 24.22 / SUPPORT 24.34 Mighty heroes fight at the forefront of battle.

[P66-04; Source: p. 66]
Before the battle, in the Muster Armies step, for each leader and support unit in your army, you can select one friendly bodyguard unit that unit can lead. That unit will then lead that bodyguard unit for the battle and form an attached unit with it.

[P66-05; Source: p. 66]
Unless otherwise stated, each bodyguard unit can only have one leader unit and one support unit attached to it.

[P66-06; Source: p. 66]
ATTACKING ATTACHED UNITS 19.02

[P66-07; Source: p. 66]
Each time an attack targets an attached unit, if that unit contains one or more bodyguard models, use the highest T characteristic of the bodyguard models in that unit while resolving that attack, even if a leader/support unit in that attached unit has a different T characteristic. If that unit only contains leader/support models, use the highest T characteristic of those models while resolving that attack instead.

[P66-08; Source: p. 66]
Rules that are triggered when a unit is destroyed are only triggered when the last model that started the battle in an attached unit is destroyed.

[P66-09; Source: p. 66]
The heroes of your army rarely fight alone. Instead, they typically lead bands of warriors, forming an attached unit that acts as a single entity. This section explains how to form attached units and how they function on the battlefield.

[P67-01; Source: p. 67]
KEYWORDS IN ATTACHED UNITS 19.03

[P67-02; Source: p. 67]
An attached unit has all of the keywords of all of its component units. As such, an attached unit is affected by any rule that applies to units with any of those keywords. Note that models in an attached unit do not gain the keywords of other models in that unit that they do not already have. Remember that attacks target units, not models.

[P67-03; Source: p. 67]
Example: An attached unit contains a leader model with the PSYKER keyword. While that model is part of that unit, that unit has the PSYKER keyword, even if the bodyguard models do not have that keyword. If that unit is attacked by a weapon with the [ANTI‑PSYKER 4+] ability, any unmodified wound roll of 4+ made against that unit is a critical wound, even if the attack itself is not allocated to that leader model.

[P67-04; Source: p. 67]
ABILITIES IN ATTACHED UNITS 19.04

[P67-05; Source: p. 67]
Abilities/rules that affect a single specified model (e.g. from an enhancement or an item of wargear) only ever apply to that model, even while part of an attached unit.

[P67-06; Source: p. 67]
Otherwise, abilities/rules that affect a unit (or models in it) apply to every model in an attached unit, until the source of that ability/rule is destroyed, as shown below.

[P67-07; Source: p. 67]
ABILITIES IN ATTACHED UNITS — table converted to labelled rows:
Source of ability/rule: Leader/support unit.
Applies to the attached unit until: The last model in that leader/support unit is destroyed.*
Source of ability/rule: Bodyguard unit (e.g. from a datasheet ability).
Applies to the attached unit until: The last model in that bodyguard unit is destroyed.
Source of ability/rule: A specific model (e.g. the bearer of an enhancement or an item of wargear).
Applies to the attached unit until: That model is destroyed.

[P67-08; Source: p. 67]
* This means leader/support units continue to benefit from their own ‘while this model is leading a unit’ abilities even after their bodyguard unit is destroyed, provided they started the battle in an attached unit.

[P67-09; Source: p. 67]
In all of the above cases, if that last model was destroyed as the result of an attack, the ability it was conferring upon the attached unit applies until the attacking unit has resolved all of its attacks.

[P67-10; Source: p. 67]
ONLY IN DEATH DOES DUTY END Leader and support units often have abilities that make the models they are leading more powerful. In the same way, some bodyguard units’ abilities can enhance the power of those leading them. The rules on the left mean that once the models conferring such effects are destroyed, that attached unit does not continue to benefit from them. Should those models later be revived, however, those abilities will once more apply to their attached unit.

PLACING UNITS IN STRATEGIC RESERVES 20.01

[P68-02; Source: p. 68]
Before the battle, in the Declare Battle Formations step, you can select one or more friendly units (excluding FORTIFICATIONS) to place in strategic reserves. Instead of setting up these units on the battlefield during deployment, place them to one side; they are strategic reserves units, and will arrive later in the battle.

[P68-03; Source: p. 68]
Unless otherwise stated, the combined points value of all of your strategic reserves units (including those embarked within TRANSPORTS that are themselves placed in strategic reserves) cannot exceed 50% of your points limit for your battle size.

[P68-04; Source: p. 68]
REPOSITIONED UNITS 20.02

[P68-05; Source: p. 68]
Some rules allow units to be removed from the battlefield and placed in strategic reserves during the battle. Units that use such rules are known as repositioned units. In addition to any other rules that apply to such units (such as where they can or cannot arrive), all of the following rules apply to them:

[P68-06; Source: p. 68]
▪ If used in the Movement phase, such rules can be used on units that have already moved that phase.
▪ A repositioned unit that is set up in the same turn in which it made an advance, fall-back or disembark move has still made an advance, fall-back or disembark move that turn.
▪ When they are removed from the battlefield, any rules that are affecting such units for a specified duration or under specified circumstances continue to affect them while that duration and/or those circumstances apply.

[P68-07; Source: p. 68]
Example: A unit that was within range of an aura ability when removed from the battlefield would no longer be affected by that aura ability if it is no longer within range of it when it makes an ingress move, but a unit that was battle-shocked when removed from the battlefield would still be battle-shocked if it makes an ingress move in the same turn.

[P68-08; Source: p. 68]
Strategic reserves are units that arrive on the battlefield at different times, either because they were held back during deployment, or because they are using special abilities to reposition themselves.

AURA ABILITIES 22.01

[P72-02; Source: p. 72]
Abilities that affect models or units within a stated range are aura abilities, and are tagged with the word ‘Aura’.

[P72-03; Source: p. 72]
While a model with an aura ability is on the battlefield, it is always within range of its own aura ability.

[P72-04; Source: p. 72]
A unit can be affected by more than one aura ability at a time, but if a unit is within range of the same aura ability more than once, that aura ability only applies to that unit once.

[P72-05; Source: p. 72]

[P72-06; Source: p. 72]
Some abilities are common to each unit that belongs to a particular faction – these are faction abilities (also known as army rules), and are listed in the Faction Abilities section of a datasheet.

[P72-07; Source: p. 72]
Unless otherwise stated, a unit’s faction abilities only apply if the army faction you selected while mustering your army matches a faction keyword listed on that unit’s datasheet.

[P72-08; Source: p. 72]
PSYCHIC ABILITIES 22.03

[P72-09; Source: p. 72]
Abilities tagged with the word ‘Psychic’ are psychic abilities. If a psychic ability causes a model to lose one or more wounds, each of those wounds is said to be inflicted by a psychic attack (this can be important for the triggering of other rules).

[P72-10; Source: p. 72]
WARGEAR ABILITIES 22.04

[P72-11; Source: p. 72]
Abilities that are gained when a unit (or one of its models) has a particular item of wargear are wargear abilities, and are listed in the Wargear Abilities section of a datasheet.

[P72-12; Source: p. 72]
If a unit has an item of wargear that has a wargear ability, that ability applies to that unit. If a model within a unit has an item of wargear that has a wargear ability, that model is the ‘bearer’ of that item of wargear and that ability applies until that model is destroyed.

ABILITIES 24.01

[P78-02; Source: p. 78]
Many units have core abilities listed on their datasheets, and weapon abilities listed in their weapon profiles.

[P78-03; Source: p. 78]
Weapon abilities are presented in square brackets with bold formatting, e.g. [BLAST]. Weapons are often described using a particular ability they have, e.g. [BLAST] weapons are those with the [BLAST] ability.

[P78-04; Source: p. 78]
If a weapon ability is followed by one or more keywords, when making attacks with that weapon, that ability only applies if the target unit has one or more of those keywords.

[P78-05; Source: p. 78]
Example: The [LETHAL HITS: VEHICLE] ability only applies to attacks that target a VEHICLE unit. The [SUSTAINED HITS 1: INFANTRY/BEASTS] ability only applies to attacks that target an INFANTRY/BEASTS unit.

[P78-06; Source: p. 78]
This section presents the most common abilities used in Warhammer 40,000. When these appear in other places, they are usually only referenced by name.

[P78-07; Source: p. 78]
DUPLICATED ABILITIES 24.02

[P78-08; Source: p. 78]
Multiple instances of the same core ability or weapon ability are not cumulative, regardless of any numbers or keywords included in them. In such cases, the controlling player must select which instance will apply at any one time. In the case of duplicated weapon abilities, this selection must be made each time that unit makes attacks, in the Select Weapons step.

[P78-09; Source: p. 78]
▪ Multiple instances of core abilities that include a number are duplicated even if that number varies. However, in the case of Scouts, you must select the lowest number not shared by every model in that unit (e.g. if every model in a unit has both Scouts 6" and Scouts 8", you could select Scouts 8" as it is shared by every model, but if a unit contains one model with Scouts 6" and five with Scouts 8", you must select Scouts 6").
▪ Multiple instances of weapon abilities that include a number (e.g. [SUSTAINED HITS 1]) are duplicated even if that number varies (e.g. the controlling player would have to select between [SUSTAINED HITS 1] and [SUSTAINED HITS 2]).
▪ Multiple instances of weapon abilities that include a keyword are duplicated even if that keyword varies (e.g. the controlling player would have to select between [ANTI‑VEHICLE 4+] and [ANTI‑INFANTRY 2+]).

STARTING STRENGTH AND HALF‑STRENGTH The number of models a unit contains at the start of the first battle round is its starting strength. The starting strength of an attached unit is the number of models that unit contains at the start of the first battle round.

[P86-04; Source: p. 86]
Some rules refer to units being below starting strength, or at – or below – half‑strength. The meaning of these terms varies depending on a unit’s starting strength, as shown below.

[P86-05; Source: p. 86]
UNIT STRENGTH — table converted to labelled rows:
BELOW STARTING STRENGTH:
Starting strength of 1: Model’s remaining wounds are less than its W characteristic.
Starting strength of 2 or more: Number of remaining models in the unit is less than its starting strength.
AT HALF-STRENGTH:
Starting strength of 1: Model’s remaining wounds are half of its W characteristic.
Starting strength of 2 or more: Number of remaining models in the unit is half of its starting strength.
BELOW HALF-STRENGTH:
Starting strength of 1: Model’s remaining wounds are less than half of its W characteristic.
Starting strength of 2 or more: Number of remaining models in the unit is less than half of its starting strength.

[P86-06; Source: p. 86]
SOME UNITS CANNOT BE AT HALF-STRENGTH If a model’s W characteristic or a unit’s starting strength cannot be evenly divided in half, that model or unit cannot be at half‑strength (but can be below half‑strength as described on the right).

[P86-07; Source: p. 86]
Example: A Captain (1 model) is attached to a unit of Intercessors (5 models). This attached unit has a starting strength of 6. If three Intercessors were destroyed, the unit would be at half‑strength. If four Intercessors were destroyed, the unit would be below half‑strength. If all of the Intercessors were destroyed, the remaining Captain would be below half‑strength, despite having his full wounds remaining.

[P88-15; Source: p. 88]

PART III — IMPLEMENTATION UNCERTAINTIES AND SOURCE DEPENDENCIES

I-01. Revival's existing-model requirement needs a declared interpretation.
Exact source wording: 'In coherency with models in that unit that started that phase on the battlefield (i.e. models that were already on the battlefield when that rule was used).'
The two time descriptions need not select the same models if a unit arrived after the phase began or if more than one revival occurs in a phase. Retain both parts of the sentence. Do not silently substitute 'any currently present model'. Before implementing that edge case, obtain a clarification or record an explicit user-approved interpretation. [Source: p. 88, Revived.]

I-02. Duplicate Scouts has special wording; its examples are explicit.
Exact source wording includes: 'in the case of Scouts, you must select the lowest number not shared by every model in that unit'. The source examples then permit Scouts 8 inches when every model shares Scouts 6 and 8 inches, and require Scouts 6 inches when one model has 6 inches and five have 8 inches. Preserve those demonstrated outcomes. The phrasing does not by itself supply a clear algorithm for every possible overlapping collection of Scouts instances. Do not invent one for unillustrated combinations without a stated interpretation. [Source: p. 78, 24.02; Scouts on p. 84.]

I-03. General dice and characteristic modifier rules are external dependencies.
This PDF gives specific modifiers, such as cover worsening BS, Plunging Fire improving BS, and HEAVY modifying a hit roll. It points to app entries including Characteristic Modifiers, Modified Characteristics, Modifying Dice Rolls, Modifying Damage, Random Characteristics and Re-rolls. Do not import a modifier cap, rounding order, minimum value, random-roll timing or general re-roll rule from the current implementation merely because it is familiar. Those details are not supplied by the references alone. Specific re-roll permissions and bans printed here must still be implemented exactly. [Source: pp. 9, 11, 17, 19, 33, 50, 73, 82, 84-86; external cross-reference panels.]

I-04. General sequencing rules are not reproduced.
The PDF explicitly orders many procedures and distinguishes active from opposing players, but lists Player's Rules and Rules Sequencing as supporting references. It does not reproduce those entries' general treatment of simultaneous effects. Keep every printed sequence; where simultaneous rules require an additional priority decision, identify that external dependency rather than adding an assumed active-player choice. [Source: pp. 8-9, 54-55, 70, 86.]

I-05. General geometric definitions and large-model placement are dependencies.
The book states concrete horizontal/vertical engagement, coherency, marker and arrival distances, and a FRAME exception. It separately references Within/Wholly Within, Closest Model/Unit, Table Quarter and Setting Up Large Models. Do not silently decide an unprovided general geometric definition or an oversized arrival exception. Preserve 'within', 'wholly within', 'more than', 'closer', 'closest', 'if possible', and 'as close as possible' rather than substituting one for another. [Source: pp. 9, 12-15, 37-38, 42, 62, 65, 69-70, 88.]

I-06. Save allocation is an ordered batch procedure, not the old procedure by default.
The source first creates allocation groups and declares their order, then rolls saves, then processes save results from lowest to highest. It checks InSv before the AP-adjusted Sv using that same result. A new current group can therefore apply different save characteristics to later results. Preserve this ordering, the CHARACTER restrictions and PRECISION's explicit current-group override. Random Damage and modifiers can require the external definitions in I-03; this source must not be supplemented by silently retaining old timing. [Source: pp. 19-23, 84.]

I-07. Destroyed-model timing has a qualified condition.
Exact source wording: 'When a model is destroyed, first resolve any rules that are triggered when it is destroyed, then it is removed from the battlefield. If any such rules apply, and if the model was destroyed as the result of an attack, unless otherwise stated, those rules are only resolved and that model is only removed after the attacking unit’s attacks have been resolved.'
Keep the condition 'If any such rules apply'; do not replace this with an unconditional rule that all destroyed models always stay until the attacking unit finishes. Attached-unit ability retention and Deadly Demise each supply related explicit wording. If a pending-destruction model raises a separate interaction not settled here, flag it rather than treating physical presence as continued eligibility or continuing abilities. [Source: pp. 66-67, 80, 87.]

I-08. General fight eligibility is distinct from an available fight type.
A unit must be eligible to be selected to fight before selecting normal or overrun fighting. An overrun's shape alone does not make a unit eligible. Pile In and Consolidate are phase-wide steps, not ordinary movement embedded in every normal fight activation. Fights First starts with the player whose turn it is, and Remaining Combats begins with the player who moved the sequence into that step. Preserve the precise switching and passing procedures. [Source: pp. 38-43, 87-88.]

I-09. Mission and faction inputs are not supplied by this core book.
Army points, complete mustering rules, mission setup/scoring/duration, playable action definitions, permitted Leader/Support attachments, complete faction abilities, and unit datasheets require their respective publications or app data. The Boyz card is an illustrative datasheet; its mention of Waaagh! does not define that faction ability. Deploy Device is explicitly labelled 'EXAMPLE ACTION (CANNOT BE USED)'. Do not manufacture these missing inputs while migrating core mechanics. [Source: pp. 4-5, 11, 28-30, 46, 52, 58, 64, 66, 72.]

I-10. Not-on-battlefield, persistent effects and wound restoration need their external scope.
Passengers are explicitly removed from the battlefield; reserves are off-table before arrival. Repositioning explicitly retains effects while their durations/circumstances continue, and revival restores specified models under its own conditions. Other general app entries named Not on the Battlefield, Persisting Rules Effects and Regaining Lost Wounds are not fully reproduced. Do not infer a complete general off-table ability, healing or persistence policy from these mentions. [Source: pp. 31, 64-65, 68-69, 86-88.]

I-11. Target changes and other weapon-profile rules are referenced, not fully supplied.
The book references Multiple Weapon Profiles and Target No Longer Eligible or Viable. Its own target-selection sequence, identical-attack batching, and timing snapshots for BLAST, CLEAVE, MELTA and RAPID FIRE are explicit and retained. If another interaction requires the contents of those app entries, obtain them instead of assuming how later attacks react to a target's changed eligibility. [Source: pp. 17, 37, 79, 83-84.]
